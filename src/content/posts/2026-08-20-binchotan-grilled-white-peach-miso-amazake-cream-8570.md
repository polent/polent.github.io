## Introduction

The transition into the warmer months calls for a dessert that balances the heat of the fire with the cooling properties of silken tofu. Today, I offer a meditation on the white peach, or Momo. By lightly searing the fruit over binchotan charcoal, we awaken its natural sugars while adding a whisper of smoke. This is softened by a cream made from our morning tofu and the natural, non-alcoholic sweetness of fermented amazake. It is a dish of quiet contrasts.

## Ingredients

* 2 ripe seasonal white peaches
* 150g fresh silken tofu, pressed for 30 minutes
* 3 tablespoons house-made sweet amazake (rice ferment)
* 1 tablespoon premium white shiro miso
* 1/2 teaspoon sudachi juice or lime juice
* 1 tablespoon toasted buckwheat groats (soba-cha)
* 4 small purple shiso micro-leaves
* A tiny pinch of flakey sea salt

## Instructions

* Prepare a small binchotan charcoal grill until the coals are glowing white and steady.
* Slice the peaches in half and remove the pits with a precise cut, keeping the skin intact to hold the juices.
* In a small stone mortar, blend the pressed silken tofu, amazake, and white miso. Work the mixture until it reaches a cloud-like consistency, then stir in the sudachi juice.
* Place the peach halves cut-side down on the grill. Sear for 2 to 3 minutes until distinct grill marks appear and the fruit begins to soften slightly.
* Transfer the warm peaches to an asymmetrical ceramic plate, resting them on their skins.
* Spoon a generous dollop of the miso-amazake cream into the hollow where the pit once rested.
* Sprinkle with toasted buckwheat for texture and a single flake of sea salt to elevate the umami.
* Garnish with a single shiso leaf to provide a sharp, herbal finish.

As you take the last bite, notice how the saltiness of the miso lingers just long enough to invite another breath. This dish is not meant to overwhelm, but to refine your palate for the season ahead. May it bring you a moment of stillness.

*Hiroshi*
