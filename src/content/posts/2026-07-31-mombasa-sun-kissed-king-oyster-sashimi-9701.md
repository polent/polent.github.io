## Introduction

Hujambo! Today, we are capturing the soul of the Indian Ocean without a single net. Inspired by the rising global love for plant-based textures, I have crafted a dish that honors the chewy, delicate mouthfeel of traditional sashimi using our highland king oyster mushrooms. We marry the earth with the sea, using a tamarind-lime infusion that echoes the salt spray of Mombasa.

## Ingredients

- 3 large king oyster mushroom stalks, sliced into 3mm rounds
- 2 tablespoons tamarind concentrate
- Juice of 2 fresh limes
- 1 teaspoon coconut aminos
- 1 small pilipili hoho (bird's eye chili), finely minced
- 1 teaspoon cold-pressed groundnut oil
- A handful of fresh coriander leaves, torn
- 1/4 red onion, cut into paper-thin translucent slices
- A pinch of flaky sea salt
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame seeds or crushed roasted groundnuts

## Instructions

- Bring a small pot of salted water to a gentle simmer and prepare a bowl of ice water on the side.
- Blanch the mushroom slices for exactly 2 minutes until they become slightly translucent but maintain a firm, chewy bite.
- Immediately plunge the slices into the ice bath to shock them, preserving that specific sashimi-like texture.
- In a small clay bowl, whisk together the tamarind concentrate, lime juice, coconut aminos, and minced pilipili to create the coastal ponzu.
- Pat the mushroom slices thoroughly dry with a clean cloth and arrange them in an overlapping pattern on a chilled plate.
- Drizzle the tamarind-lime mixture evenly over the mushrooms and let them sit for 5 minutes to absorb the flavors.
- Scatter the red onion slices and coriander leaves over the top, then finish with a light drizzle of groundnut oil.
- Sprinkle with flaky sea salt and toasted sesame seeds just before serving.

Serve this chilled as the sun dips below the horizon, and let the bright acidity of the lime and the deep hum of the tamarind transport you to my kitchen by the shore. It is a modern Swahili classic in the making.
