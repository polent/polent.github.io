## Introduction

In my Tuscan village, winter cooking is all about taking what the frost-kissed orto gives us and elevating it in the blazing heat of the oven. This artisan pizza pairs a chewy, blistered sourdough crust with a velvety cannellini bean cream, tender charred cavolo nero, and fragrant pan-roasted forest mushrooms, finished with our peppery olio nuovo.

## Ingredients

* 500g high-protein bread flour (Tipo 0 or Manitoba)
* 350ml warm filtered water
* 100g active sourdough starter or 3g active dry yeast
* 10g fine sea salt
* 15ml extra virgin olive oil plus more for finishing
* 1 cup cooked cannellini beans, drained
* 1 whole head of garlic, roasted until sweet and creamy
* 2 tablespoons unsweetened plant milk or warm water
* 1 sprig fresh rosemary, needles stripped and finely minced
* 1 bunch fresh cavolo nero (Tuscan kale), stems stripped and leaves torn
* 200g wild mushrooms (chanterelles and king oysters), torn or sliced
* 1 small shallot, thinly sliced
* 30g toasted walnuts, roughly crushed
* 1 tablespoon nutritional yeast
* Flaky sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper

## Instructions

* Whisk the warm water and active sourdough starter together in a wide terracotta bowl, fold in the flour until shaggy, and rest for 30 minutes to autolyse before working in the sea salt and olive oil.
* Perform three series of stretch-and-folds spaced 30 minutes apart, then transfer the covered dough to the refrigerator for a slow cold fermentation of 24 to 48 hours.
* Purée the cooked cannellini beans, roasted garlic cloves, minced rosemary, plant milk, a glug of extra virgin olive oil, and sea salt in a small blender until completely smooth and velvety.
* Heat a cast-iron pan with a drizzle of oil over high heat and sear the mushrooms and shallots until deeply browned and caramelized, seasoning lightly with salt and pepper.
* Gently massage the torn cavolo nero with a few drops of olive oil and a pinch of salt to soften the raw fiber.
* Position a baking steel or heavy pizza stone in your oven and preheat at maximum temperature (250°C / 485°F or higher) for one hour.
* Divide dough into two portions and gently stretch one ball across a semolina-dusted wooden peel, preserving the airy fermented rim.
* Spread a generous base of the cannellini garlic crema, then scatter the seared mushrooms and massaged cavolo nero across the top.
* Slide the pizza onto the blistering hot stone and bake for 7 to 9 minutes until the cornicione is charred with leopard spots and the kale edges are crisp.
* Finish directly out of the oven with crushed toasted walnuts, a dusting of nutritional yeast, cracked black pepper, and a swirl of raw extra virgin olive oil.

Pull this bubbling pie from the oven, slice it while the crust still crackles beneath the blade, and drizzle generously with fresh cold-pressed olive oil. It is rustic, deeply nourishing, and true to the spirit of cucina povera: taking humble garden staples and turning them into pure magic.
