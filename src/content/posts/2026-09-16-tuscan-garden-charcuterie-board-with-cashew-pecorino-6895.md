## Introduction

Benvenuti to my hill town kitchen. Today, we bring the spirit of the Tuscan aperitivo to your table. In my garden, we believe that a tagliere—our version of a charcuterie board—should tell a story of the season. This board is a marriage of the old ways and the new: a fermented cashew cheese that mimics the sharp tang of a young pecorino, paired with a smoky lentil and walnut terrine that honors the rustic textures of our traditional salami. It is simple, generous, and best enjoyed as the sun begins to set over the cypresses.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups raw cashews, soaked in water for at least 4 hours
* 2 tablespoons nutritional yeast
* 1 tablespoon white miso paste
* 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
* 1/2 teaspoon sea salt
* 1 cup cooked brown lentils, well-drained
* 1/2 cup toasted walnuts, finely chopped
* 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
* 1/2 teaspoon toasted fennel seeds, crushed
* 1/4 teaspoon freshly cracked black pepper
* Fresh focaccia or sourdough bread, sliced
* A handful of marinated green olives
* 1 ripe pear, thinly sliced
* A small bunch of grapes or fresh figs
* Finest quality cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil for drizzling
* Sprigs of fresh rosemary and thyme for garnish

## Instructions

* To make the cashew pecorino, drain and rinse the soaked cashews. Blend them with nutritional yeast, miso, lemon juice, and salt until completely smooth and creamy. Transfer to a small bowl lined with cheesecloth or parchment, and chill in the refrigerator for at least 4 hours to firm up.
* To prepare the lentil-walnut terrine, place the cooked lentils in a bowl and mash them slightly with a fork. Fold in the chopped walnuts, smoked paprika, crushed fennel seeds, salt, and pepper. Form the mixture into a tight log shape using parchment paper and chill alongside the cheese until firm.
* When ready to serve, find a beautiful wooden board. Place the cashew pecorino in the center and dust with a little black pepper.
* Slice the lentil-walnut terrine into rounds and arrange them nearby.
* Add the slices of fresh pear and grapes to provide a sweet contrast to the savory elements.
* Fill the remaining spaces with small bowls of olives and piles of torn focaccia.
* Garnish the board with fresh rosemary and thyme sprigs from the garden.
* Just before serving, drizzle the cheese and the bread generously with your best extra virgin olive oil.

Pour yourself a glass of chilled Vermentino and let the evening linger. This board is not about perfection, but about the joy of sharing what the earth provides. Remember to use the best extra virgin olive oil you can find; it is the thread that sews these flavors together. Buon appetito!

*Isabella*
