## Introduction

The dusk over Mombasa smells of brine, woodsmoke, and sweet coconut milk simmering across seaside verandas. Tonight, we pair green highland matoke bananas with tender pigeon peas, braising them in a fragrant cardamom-tamarind coconut broth that balances deep coastal heat with gentle acidity.

## Ingredients

- 4 green cooking bananas (matoke), peeled and cut into thick rounds
- 1.5 cups cooked green pigeon peas (mbaazi)
- 1.5 cups freshly pressed thick coconut milk
- 1 cup light coconut milk or vegetable broth
- 2 tablespoons cold-pressed coconut oil
- 1 red onion, finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, grated
- 1 thumb fresh ginger, minced
- 1 fresh pilipili or bird's eye chile, slit lengthwise
- 1 teaspoon whole cumin seeds, lightly bruised
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground green cardamom seeds
- 1/4 teaspoon fenugreek powder
- 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1.5 tablespoons tamarind paste diluted in 3 tablespoons warm water
- 1 teaspoon sea salt, adjusted to taste
- 1 cup fresh sukuma wiki (collard greens), finely ribboned for garnish
- 1 small red onion, 1 plum tomato, and fresh coriander tossed with lime juice (quick kachumbari) for serving

## Instructions

1. Heat a dry cast-iron tava or skillet over medium-high flame and char the banana rounds for 2 to 3 minutes per side until flecked with deep grill marks; set aside.
2. In a heavy-bottomed clay pot or Dutch oven, warm the coconut oil over medium heat and temper the cumin and cardamom seeds until aromatic and crackling.
3. Add the diced onion, minced ginger, garlic, and slit pilipili, sautéing gently until the onions soften and turn translucent.
4. Stir in the turmeric, fenugreek, and sea salt, coating the aromatics before pouring in the light coconut milk and tamarind water.
5. Add the charred matoke rounds and cooked pigeon peas, bringing the liquid to a gentle, steady simmer.
6. Cover and cook over low heat for 15 minutes, allowing the green bananas to become tender while holding their shape.
7. Uncover, pour in the rich thick coconut milk, and stir in the ribboned sukuma wiki, simmering very gently for another 3 to 4 minutes without letting the coconut boil vigorously.
8. Remove from the heat, rest for 5 minutes, and serve warm topped with freshly tossed lime kachumbari.

Ladle this stew into your favorite clay bowl, top with crisp kachumbari, and let the gentle steam carry you straight to the spice routes of the Swahili shore.
