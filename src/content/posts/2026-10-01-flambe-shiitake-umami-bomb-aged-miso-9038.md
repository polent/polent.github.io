## Introduction

Welcome to my sanctuary. Today, we dance between the quiet discipline of the Kyoto temple and the spirited warmth of the modern gastro-pub. This dish is a meditation on fire and earth. We take the humble, thick-capped Shiitake, treat it with the reverence of a steak, and introduce it to the dramatic kiss of a shochu flame. It is a bridge between worlds—one silent, one roaring, both deeply nourishing.

## Ingredients

* 6 large, thick-capped Shiitake mushrooms (donko grade)
* 200g firm house-made silken tofu, pressed and cubed
* 100ml Kombu dashi (cold-steeped for 12 hours)
* 2 tablespoons 3-year aged red miso
* 1 tablespoon aged tamari
* 1 teaspoon fresh ginger juice
* 2 tablespoons high-quality Shochu (for flambé)
* 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
* Micro-shiso and a single seasonal maple leaf for garnish
* A pinch of sansho pepper

## Instructions

* Score the caps of the Shiitake mushrooms in a fine diamond pattern to allow the heat to penetrate evenly.
* Prepare the glaze by whisking the aged miso, tamari, ginger juice, and a splash of kombu dashi until smooth.
* Light the binchotan charcoal until it glows with a steady, white heat. Lightly brush the mushrooms and tofu with sesame oil.
* Grill the mushrooms cap-side down first, then the tofu, until a delicate char forms and the aroma of the forest fills the air.
* Move the ingredients to a pre-heated iron pan. Pour the remaining glaze over them, allowing it to bubble and caramelize for thirty seconds.
* Carefully pour the shochu over the mushrooms and ignite with a long match. Let the blue flame dance until it extinguishes naturally, concentrating the umami.
* Plate immediately on an asymmetric wabi-sabi ceramic dish. Dust with a whisper of sansho pepper and garnish with shiso.

As the flame dies down, it leaves behind a whisper of smoke and a concentrated sweetness in the miso. This is the essence of shun—capturing the forest's depth at its peak. I hope this dish brings a moment of warmth and clarity to your table. Enjoy the silence between the bites.

*Hiroshi*
