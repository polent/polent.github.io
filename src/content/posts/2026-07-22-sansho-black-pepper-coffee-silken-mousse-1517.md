## Introduction

In the stillness of the Kyoto morning, the air often carries the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke. Today, I am moved by the marriage of deep, roasted coffee and the bracing heat of pepper. This dish is not merely a sweet; it is a meditation on the contrast between the dark, bitter notes of the bean and the electric vibration of the spice. By using the silk of the morning's tofu, we create a vessel for these flavors to dance without weight.

## Ingredients

* 300g freshly crafted silken tofu, drained of excess water
* 60ml concentrated dark roast cold-brew coffee
* 2 tablespoons Okinawan kokuto (black sugar) or maple syrup
* 1 teaspoon aged tamari
* 1/2 teaspoon freshly cracked black pepper (kuro-kosho)
* 1/4 teaspoon ground sansho pepper
* A small pinch of fine sea salt
* Shiso flowers or a single kinome leaf for garnish

## Instructions

* Gently wrap the silken tofu in a clean sarashi cloth and let it rest for ten minutes to express the heaviest moisture.
* In a suribachi (Japanese mortar), combine the tofu with the tamari and black sugar, grinding with a pestle until the texture is like heavy silk.
* Slowly pour in the cold-brew coffee while continuing to stir in a circular motion, ensuring the coffee is fully integrated into the cream.
* Fold in the freshly cracked black pepper and the sea salt, allowing the aromatic oils to begin infusing the tofu.
* Spoon the mixture into small, imperfect ceramic bowls, leaving the surface with a natural, unstudied texture.
* Just before serving, dust the top with a fine veil of sansho pepper to capture its volatile citrus aroma.
* Place a single kinome leaf or a few shiso flowers off-center to honor the beauty of asymmetry.

As you finish, let the lingering tingle of the sansho remind you of the transition between seasons. The pepper warms the blood while the coffee clears the mind. Simple, focused, and present.

*Hiroshi*
