## Introduction

Benvenuti to my kitchen in the heart of the Tuscan hills. Today, the scent of woodsmoke hangs in the crisp air, inspiring a sauce that honors the ancient hearth. This Smoked Charred Pepper and Beluga Lentil Sugo is my vegan tribute to the rich, deep flavors of the season, blending the sweetness of fire-roasted peppers with the grounding earthiness of tiny black lentils.

## Ingredients

* 3 large red bell peppers
* 1 cup dried black beluga lentils, rinsed
* 1 large yellow onion, finely diced
* 2 cloves garlic, smashed and peeled
* 1 tablespoon smoked paprika (pimentón)
* 1/2 teaspoon smoked sea salt
* 1 tablespoon double-concentrated tomato paste
* 1/2 cup dry red wine, such as Chianti
* 4 cups vegetable broth or water
* 1 sprig fresh rosemary
* New-press extra virgin olive oil for finishing

## Instructions

* Char the red peppers over an open gas flame or under a hot broiler until the skins are completely blackened and blistered.
* Place the charred peppers in a bowl and cover with a plate for 10 minutes to steam; then peel away the charred skins, remove seeds, and finely dice the flesh.
* In a heavy-bottomed terracotta or cast iron pot, warm a generous splash of olive oil over medium heat and sauté the onion until soft and translucent.
* Stir in the garlic, smoked paprika, and tomato paste, cooking for 2 minutes until the paste darkens and smells toasted.
* Pour in the red wine to deglaze the pan, scraping up any flavorful bits from the bottom.
* Add the diced peppers, lentils, rosemary sprig, and vegetable broth; bring to a gentle boil.
* Reduce the heat to low and simmer uncovered for about 30 to 35 minutes, or until the lentils are tender and the sauce has thickened significantly.
* Remove the rosemary sprig and the garlic cloves; season with smoked sea salt and a vigorous stir of extra virgin olive oil before serving.

Ladle this generous sugo over hand-cut pasta or a thick slice of grilled sourdough. It is a dish that speaks of the garden and the fire, simple yet profound. I hope it brings as much warmth to your table as it does to mine. Buon appetito!

*Isabella*
