## Introduction

Hey there! Today we’re bringing the soul of a foggy coastal pub right into the sunshine. Think of these as the ultimate Northern California handhelds—meaty grilled mushrooms drenched in a sun-drenched adobo, tucked into toasted masa buns. It’s smoky, bright, and perfect for sharing with a crisp glass of local pét-nat or a chilled verjus sparkler. This is unfussy coastal comfort at its finest.

## Ingredients

* 1 lb King Trumpet and Oyster mushrooms, torn into bite-sized strips
* 2 tbsp Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
* 1/4 cup chipotle in adobo sauce, pureed
* 1 tsp smoked paprika
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 6 small slider-sized masa buns or sourdough rolls
* 2 large heirloom tomatoes, sliced 1/2-inch thick
* 1 tbsp verjus or fresh lemon juice
* 1/2 cup vegan avocado-oil aioli
* 1 tbsp non-pareil capers, roughly chopped
* 1/4 cup fresh cilantro and Italian parsley, finely minced
* 1 ripe Hass avocado, thinly sliced
* Handful of pickled Fresno chiles
* Flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* Preheat a heavy cast-iron skillet or an outdoor grill over medium-high heat. If you can, use almond wood for a sweet, nutty smoke profile.
* In a large bowl, whisk together the olive oil, adobo sauce, smoked paprika, and minced garlic.
* Toss the torn mushrooms in the adobo mixture until every crevice is coated.
* Sear the mushrooms in the hot skillet or on a grill basket until the edges are crispy and charred, about 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
* While the mushrooms cook, place the heirloom tomato slices directly on the grill or a separate hot pan for 60 seconds per side until they show char marks. Transfer to a small bowl and mash gently with the verjus and a pinch of salt to create a chunky, rustic jam.
* In a small ramekin, fold the chopped capers and fresh herbs into the vegan aioli.
* Split the buns and toast them lightly until golden.
* Build the sliders by spreading a thick layer of the herb-caper aioli on the bottom bun. Pile on the smoky mushrooms, followed by a spoonful of the charred tomato jam.
* Top with a slice of avocado and a few pickled Fresno chiles for a hit of acid and heat.
* Serve immediately while the mushrooms are still sizzling.

There you have it—a little piece of the Pacific coast on a plate. This dish is all about the layers: the deep wood-fired char, the sweetness of the heirloom tomatoes, and that creamy, briny herb finish. Pull up a chair, grab a cold drink, and enjoy the peak of the season. Stay sun-drenched, friends!
