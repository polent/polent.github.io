## Introduction

Hujambo! Step into my kitchen, where the warmth of the charcoal jiko meets the cool breeze of Mombasa. Today, I am bringing you a snack that tells a story of travel and tradition. We are taking the hearty njahi black bean of the highlands and wrapping it in the golden, spiced crust of the coast. It is a bite of home, elevated with the fiery soul of berbere and the tang of fresh tamarind. This is more than a snack; it is the Indian Ocean trade winds meeting the red soil of our ancestors.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup njahi (black beans), soaked overnight and boiled until tender
* 1 cup cassava, peeled, boiled, and finely mashed
* 1 large red onion, finely diced
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated
* 2 teaspoons berbere spice blend
* 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
* 1 small pilipili chile, finely chopped (adjust for heat)
* 1/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
* 12-15 vegan samosa pastry sheets or spring roll wrappers
* Coconut oil for shallow frying
* 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
* 1 tablespoon jaggery or brown sugar
* A squeeze of fresh lime juice
* Sea salt to taste

## Instructions

* Heat a small amount of coconut oil in a pan over medium heat and toast the cumin seeds until they pop.
* Add the diced onions, garlic, and ginger, sautéing until the onions are translucent and fragrant.
* Stir in the boiled njahi beans, mashed cassava, berbere spice, cardamom, and chopped pilipili. Mash slightly with the back of a spoon to create a cohesive but textured filling.
* Season with salt, stir in the fresh cilantro, then remove from heat and let the mixture cool completely.
* Take a pastry sheet and fold it into a cone shape. Fill with two tablespoons of the bean and cassava mixture, then seal the edges using a flour-and-water paste.
* In a heavy-bottomed pan or tava, heat coconut oil and shallow fry the samosas until they are golden brown and crispy on all sides. Drain on paper towels.
* Prepare the glaze by whisking the tamarind paste, jaggery, and lime juice in a small saucepan over low heat until thickened and glossy.
* Drizzle the warm tamarind glaze over the samosas or serve it on the side with a fresh bowl of kachumbari relish.

These little parcels are best enjoyed shared, perhaps on a veranda as the light turns golden. The crunch of the pastry and the earthy richness of the beans will transport you straight to the heart of the Swahili coast. Serve them warm, and let the scent of cardamom linger in the air. Karibu chakula!

*Nia*
