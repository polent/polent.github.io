## Introduction

Karibu! Today we honor the dawn with a plate that bridges the red soil of the highlands and the salty spray of the Mombasa coast. We take the humble cassava and the noble king oyster mushroom, shredding them into a texture that catches every drop of my spiced coconut reduction. This Shredded Cassava and Mushroom Sunrise Skillet is a breakfast that feels like the first light over the Indian Ocean—hearty, aromatic, and deeply soul-stirring.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups king oyster mushrooms, shredded with a fork
* 1 large cassava root, peeled and coarsely shredded
* 1 bunch sukuma wiki (collard greens), finely shredded
* 1/2 cup fresh coconut milk, pressed this morning
* 1 tbsp berbere spice blend
* 1 tsp ground cumin
* 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 inch fresh ginger, grated
* 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
* 1 fresh pilipili chile, minced
* Juice of 1 lime
* 2 tbsp cold-pressed coconut oil
* Sea salt to taste
* Fresh cilantro and kachumbari for garnish

## Instructions

* Rinse the shredded cassava in cold water to remove excess starch and squeeze very dry using a clean kitchen towel to ensure maximum crispiness.
* Heat the coconut oil in a heavy tava or cast-iron skillet over a medium-high jiko or stove.
* Spread the shredded cassava in an even layer in the skillet and cook without stirring for 4-5 minutes until the bottom is golden and crisp, then flip and break into smaller, crispy clusters.
* Add the shredded mushrooms to the skillet, stirring them into the cassava until they begin to brown and develop a meaty, chewy texture.
* Toss in the onions, garlic, ginger, and pilipili chile, sautéing until the aromatics are fragrant and soft.
* Sprinkle the berbere, cumin, and cardamom over the mixture, stirring well to ensure every shred is coated in the deep red spices.
* Fold in the finely shredded sukuma wiki and immediately pour over the fresh coconut milk.
* Allow the mixture to simmer for 3-4 minutes until the greens are tender and the coconut milk has thickened into a rich, savory glaze that coats the shreds.
* Remove from the heat and finish with a generous squeeze of fresh lime juice and a handful of cilantro.
* Serve immediately in warm bowls, topped with a spoonful of fresh kachumbari relish.

As you finish the last bite of this golden, crispy, and creamy creation, let the warmth of the berbere linger like a coastal sunset. This is the energy of the coast, captured in a bowl. Serve this to your loved ones alongside a steaming cup of spiced ginger chai for the perfect start to the day.
