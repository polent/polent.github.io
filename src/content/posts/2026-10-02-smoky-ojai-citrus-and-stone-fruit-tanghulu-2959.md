## Introduction

Hey there! Today, I am bringing a bit of that coastal boardwalk energy back to the kitchen, but with a refined wine-country edge. We are taking the candied street food trend—inspired by the shimmering glass-crunch of Tanghulu—and grounding it with smoky Central Valley chiles and bright Ojai citrus. It is sweet, shattering, and just a little bit fiery, perfectly capturing a sunlit afternoon in Northern California. This is unfussy, seasonal, and meant to be shared right after the sugar sets.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup organic granulated cane sugar
* 1/2 cup filtered water
* 1 tablespoon verjus or fresh lemon juice
* 2 Ojai tangerines, peeled and segmented (ensure all pith is removed)
* 4 small Central Valley apricots or plums, halved and pitted
* 1 teaspoon smoked ancho chile powder
* 1/2 teaspoon flaky sea salt harvested from the Pacific
* 6-8 bamboo skewers

## Instructions

* Line a baking sheet with parchment paper or a silicone mat and set it aside.
* Thread the tangerine segments and stone fruit halves onto the bamboo skewers, making sure the fruit is completely dry to ensure the sugar sticks.
* In a small, heavy-bottomed saucepan, combine the cane sugar, water, and verjus.
* Place over medium-high heat. Do not stir the mixture; instead, gently swirl the pan if needed to incorporate the sugar.
* Use a candy thermometer to monitor the temperature. Heat until the syrup reaches 300°F (hard-crack stage).
* Working quickly and carefully, tilt the pan and dip each fruit skewer into the hot syrup, rotating it to create a thin, even coat.
* Immediately sprinkle a tiny pinch of the ancho chile powder and flaky sea salt over the wet sugar shell.
* Place the finished skewers onto the prepared parchment paper.
* Let them cool and harden for about 3 to 5 minutes at room temperature before serving.

There you have it—a crunchy, shimmering bite of California sunshine. These skewers are best enjoyed immediately while the sugar shell is glass-like and the fruit inside stays cool and juicy. It is that perfect balance of the Central Valley harvest and coastal flair. Catch you at the next market!
