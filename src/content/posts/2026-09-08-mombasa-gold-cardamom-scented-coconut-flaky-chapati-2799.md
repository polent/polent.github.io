## Introduction

Welcome to my kitchen by the shore, where the scent of morning coconut milk meets the warmth of the charcoal jiko. Today, we are crafting a side dish that is the heartbeat of any Swahili meal: a multi-layered, flaky chapati, kissed with the floral heat of cardamom and the richness of fresh coconut oil. These are not just flatbreads; they are maps of the spice trade, designed to pull apart in silken threads and scoop up the richest stews.

## Ingredients

- 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for dusting
- 1 teaspoon sea salt
- 1 teaspoon freshly ground cardamom seeds
- 1 tablespoon organic cane sugar
- 1 cup warm coconut milk, freshly pressed if possible
- 4 tablespoons melted virgin coconut oil, plus extra for layering
- Vegetable oil for pan-frying

## Instructions

- In a large earthenware bowl, whisk together the flour, salt, cardamom, and sugar until well combined.
- Create a well in the center and pour in the warm coconut milk and 2 tablespoons of the melted coconut oil.
- Mix by hand until a shaggy dough forms, then turn onto a floured surface and knead for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
- Cover the dough with a damp cloth and let it rest for 30 minutes to allow the gluten to relax.
- Divide the dough into 8 equal-sized balls. Roll each ball out into a very thin, translucent circle.
- Brush the surface of the circle generously with melted coconut oil. 
- Roll the circle tightly like a cigar to create a long rope, then coil that rope into a spiral (like a snail shell), tucking the end underneath.
- Let these coils rest for another 15 minutes; this is the secret to the flakiness.
- On a lightly floured surface, roll each coil out into a circle about 6 inches wide.
- Heat a tava or heavy cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Place a chapati on the dry pan for 30 seconds until tiny bubbles appear, then flip.
- Spread a teaspoon of oil over the surface, flip again, and press down with a flat spatula. Repeat until both sides have beautiful golden-brown charred spots and the layers puff up.
- Stack the finished chapatis in a clean kitchen towel to keep them soft and pliable.

Serve these warm, straight from the tava, while the steam still carries the scent of the highlands. They are perfect for scooping up njahi black bean stews or simply enjoyed with a cup of spiced chai as the sun sets over the Indian Ocean. Karibu chakula!
