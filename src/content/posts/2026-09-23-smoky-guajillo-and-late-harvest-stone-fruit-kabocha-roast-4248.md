## Introduction

There is something magical about the way the afternoon sun hits the coastline this time of year, and it always makes me crave a dish that feels grounded yet bright. Today, I am leaning into that golden hour energy with a tender roast that swaps the traditional for something straight from the soil. We are taking a gorgeous, dense Kabocha squash and treating it with the respect of a centerpiece, basting it in a smoky guajillo and late-season stone fruit glaze that screams California autumn. It is unfussy, deeply flavorful, and meant to be shared on a sunlit patio with a crisp glass of local white wine.

## Ingredients

* 1 large Kabocha squash, halved and seeded, cut into thick 2-inch wedges
* 3 tablespoons Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
* 2 dried guajillo chiles, stemmed and seeded
* 1/2 cup ripe plums or nectarines, pitted and chopped
* 2 tablespoons verjus or fresh lemon juice
* 1 tablespoon maple syrup or agave
* 2 cloves garlic, smashed
* 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
* 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds, toasted
* Sea salt and cracked black pepper to taste
* 1/4 cup toasted pepitas (pumpkin seeds)
* 1/4 cup fresh cilantro and mint leaves, torn
* 1 tablespoon preserved lemon rind, finely minced

## Instructions

* Preheat your oven to 425F. If you have an outdoor grill with almond wood or oak, that is even better for that authentic smoky finish.
* In a small saucepan, lightly toast the guajillo chiles over medium heat until fragrant, then cover with hot water and soak for 10 minutes until soft.
* In a blender, combine the soaked chiles, chopped stone fruit, olive oil, verjus, garlic, maple syrup, smoked paprika, and cumin seeds. Blend until completely smooth to create your adobo glaze.
* Arrange the Kabocha wedges on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Generously brush each wedge with the adobo glaze, ensuring they are well-coated. Season with sea salt and black pepper.
* Roast for 35 to 40 minutes, flipping halfway through and applying more glaze, until the squash is tender and the edges are beautifully charred and caramelized.
* While the roast is finishing, toss the toasted pepitas with the preserved lemon and fresh herbs.
* Transfer the tender squash wedges to a large wooden platter. Top with the herb and pepita mixture and a final drizzle of olive oil before serving warm.

This roast is a reminder that the best meals do not need to be complicated; they just need to honor the ingredients. The smoky heat from the chiles paired with the jammy sweetness of the plums creates a profile that is pure West Coast soul. I hope this brings a little bit of that Ojai sunshine and Pacific breeze to your table today. Enjoy every bite, and I will see you at the market tomorrow!

*Emily*
