## Introduction

Hallo aus dem Schwarzwald! Today we are tapping into the ancient, grounding magic of clay-pot braising. When earth meets earthen vessel, something extraordinary happens to humble winter roots. We steep dense black salsify, smoked beech mushrooms, and hearty shallots in a rich, deeply savory marinade driven by fermented malt beer, roasted barley miso, and crushed spruce needles. Simmered slow and sealed tight in natural clay, the marinade reduces into a glossy, forest-scented glaze that wraps around every vegetable like a warm woolen blanket.

## Ingredients

* 400 g black salsify (Schwarzwurzeln), peeled and cut into 5 cm batons (kept in acidulated water until ready)
* 200 g king oyster or brown beech mushrooms, torn into bite-sized pieces
* 4 medium shallots, peeled and halved lengthwise
* 2 small parsnips, peeled and sliced into thick wedges
* 3 cloves garlic, smashed
* 200 ml German dark malt beer (Dunkelbier or Malzbier)
* 2 tablespoons dark barley miso or rye miso
* 2 tablespoons cold-pressed rapeseed oil
* 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar or lacto-fermented berry brine
* 1 tablespoon maple syrup or beet molasses
* 1 teaspoon dried juniper berries, lightly crushed
* 1 sprig fresh rosemary
* 1/2 teaspoon freshly crushed spruce tips or pine needles (food grade)
* 1 teaspoon whole grain German mustard
* Sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste
* Fresh flat-leaf parsley for garnish

## Instructions

* Soak your unglazed clay pot (Römertopf or clay baker) in cold water for at least 20 minutes if using traditional earthenware.
* Whisk together the dark malt beer, dark miso, rapeseed oil, apple cider vinegar, beet molasses, crushed juniper berries, spruce needles, and whole grain mustard in a large bowl to form the marinade.
* Toss the prepared black salsify, parsnip wedges, mushrooms, shallots, and smashed garlic directly into the marinade, coating every piece thoroughly. Let rest at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow the roots to absorb the malt and woodsy aromatics.
* Transfer the entire marinated mixture, including all the liquid and the fresh rosemary sprig, into the pre-soaked clay pot. Season lightly with sea salt and cracked black pepper.
* Cover with the clay lid and place into a cold oven. Set the temperature to 190°C (375°F) and bake for 50 minutes, letting the pot heat gradually.
* Remove the lid carefully, baste the roots with the bubbling dark glaze, and bake uncovered for an additional 15 minutes until the salsify is fork-tender and the marinade has reduced to a sticky, savory sheen.
* Discard the woody rosemary stem, scatter freshly chopped flat-leaf parsley over the top, and serve piping hot straight from the clay pot.

Ladle this steaming forest pot straight onto warm bowls of rustic spelt mash or alongside crusty sourdough to soak up every last drop of that dark, malted glaze. It is honest, grounded, and sings of the misty woodlands. Guten Appetit!
