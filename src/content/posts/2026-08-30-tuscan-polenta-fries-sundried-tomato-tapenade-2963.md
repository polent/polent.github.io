## Introduction

Benvenuti! This dish is a love letter to the cucina povera, where we transform the humblest cornmeal into a celebration worthy of a piazza feast. In the spirit of today's reduced food festival, we honor the philosophy of using what we have—stale-stored polenta and the intense, sun-drenched preserves of last season—to create something crunchy, salty, and soulful.

## Ingredients

- 250g coarse cornmeal (polenta)
- 1 liter water
- 1 tsp sea salt
- 3 sprigs fresh rosemary, needles removed
- 100g sun-dried tomatoes in oil, drained (reserve the oil)
- 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
- 1 small clove garlic, peeled
- Zest of one organic lemon
- Extra virgin olive oil for frying

## Instructions

- Bring the water to a rolling boil in a heavy-bottomed pot and add the sea salt.
- Slowly whisk in the cornmeal in a steady stream to avoid lumps, then reduce the heat to low.
- Stir the polenta regularly with a wooden spoon for about 35 to 40 minutes until it is thick and pulls away cleanly from the sides of the pot.
- Pour the hot polenta onto a parchment-lined tray, smoothing it to a thickness of 2cm. Allow it to cool and set completely for at least one hour.
- While the polenta sets, create the tapenade by pounding the sun-dried tomatoes, capers, garlic, and lemon zest in a mortar and pestle until a rustic paste forms. Stir in a little of the reserved tomato oil for silkiness.
- Once the polenta is firm, cut it into thick batons or wedges.
- Heat a generous layer of olive oil in a wide skillet. Toss in the rosemary needles to infuse the oil until they are fragrant.
- Fry the polenta batons in batches until each side is deeply golden and remarkably crispy. Drain them on a clean kitchen cloth.
- Serve the warm fries immediately with the tapenade on the side for dipping.

Serve these golden treasures while the sun sets over the hills, perhaps with a glass of chilled Vermentino. It is the taste of the Tuscan spring, shared with love and a spirit that knows nothing is ever truly wasted. Buon appetito!
