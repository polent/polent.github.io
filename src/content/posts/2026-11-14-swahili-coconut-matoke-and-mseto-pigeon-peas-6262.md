## Introduction

Today calls for something rustic yet breezy, grounding yet light on the palate. I have brought together sweet coastal green bananas with tender pigeon peas, braising them gently in hand-pressed coconut milk infused with toasted cardamom, ground cumin, and sour tamarind. Finished with a fiery crunch of crushed groundnuts and crisp ribboned sukuma wiki, it is the spirit of Mombasa midday in a single earthenware bowl.

## Ingredients

- 4 green cooking bananas (matoke), peeled and sliced into thick rounds
- 1.5 cups fresh or cooked pigeon peas (mbaazi)
- 1 can (400 ml) freshly pressed thick coconut milk
- 1 tablespoon cold-pressed coconut oil
- 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 1 inch fresh ginger, minced
- 1 green pilipili (bird\'s eye chile), pierced with a knife
- 1 teaspoon whole cumin seeds
- 4 green cardamom pods, lightly cracked
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1 tablespoon tamarind paste dissolved in 3 tablespoons warm water
- 1 cup finely shredded sukuma wiki (collard greens)
- 3 tablespoons roasted red-skin groundnuts (peanuts), roughly crushed
- Fresh lime wedges and chopped fresh coriander for serving
- Sea salt to taste

## Instructions

- Heat the coconut oil in a clay pot or heavy-bottomed skillet over medium heat, tossing in cumin seeds and cracked cardamom until fragrant and sizzling.
- Add the sliced red onion, minced ginger, and garlic, sautéing gently until the onions soften and begin to turn translucent.
- Stir in the ground turmeric and the pierced pilipili chile to release their aromas without overpowering the base.
- Tip in the sliced green bananas and pigeon peas, tossing to coat them completely in the spiced oil.
- Pour over the thick coconut milk, the tamarind water, and season with sea salt, bringing the liquid to a lively simmer.
- Lower the heat, cover with an earthenware lid, and braise gently for 15 to 18 minutes until the matoke are tender like braised potatoes but hold their shape.
- Uncover, fold in the shredded sukuma wiki greens, and allow them to wilt into the silky broth for just 2 minutes.
- Remove from heat, scatter crushed roasted groundnuts and freshly torn coriander across the top, and finish with a squeeze of fresh lime juice.

Serve this warm while the sea breeze cools down the afternoon, accompanied by a quick charred chapati or a wedge of fermented cassava bread. Every spoonful holds the warmth of our coastal charcoal fires and the vibrant acidity of the Swahili shore.
