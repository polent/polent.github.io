## Introduction

Welcome. Today, we listen to the voice of the yuzu at its absolute shun. This dish is a quiet meditation on the transition from winter’s depth to the first breath of spring. By hollowing the fruit and lightly kissing it with binchotan flame, we release an aroma that clears the mind before the first bite of our morning-made silken tofu.

## Ingredients

- 2 large firm-skinned organic yuzu or sudachi
- 200g fresh silken tofu crafted this morning
- 100ml ichiban kombu dashi cold-extracted for 12 hours
- 1 tablespoon artisanal white miso fermented in-house
- 1 teaspoon aged tamari
- 1 teaspoon organic mirin
- 1 small segment of dekopon citrus for natural sweetness
- 2 micro-shiso leaves or hanaho shiso flowers
- A pinch of fresh yuzu zest

## Instructions

- Carefully slice the top third of the yuzu to create a lid and hollow out the fruit using a small spoon, reserving two teaspoons of the juice.
- Hold the empty yuzu shells with bamboo tongs over glowing binchotan charcoal for 20 seconds until the skin expresses its oils and smells toasted.
- In a small copper pot, whisk the white miso, tamari, and mirin over low heat until the mixture thickens into a glossy glaze.
- Warm the silken tofu gently in the kombu dashi at exactly 70 degrees Celsius, ensuring the texture remains like custard.
- Place a portion of the warm tofu inside each charred yuzu shell and add one small segment of dekopon.
- Spoon the miso glaze over the tofu and place the yuzu lid slightly ajar.
- Steam the entire vessel for exactly three minutes to infuse the tofu with the citrus fragrance.
- Finish with a drop of fresh yuzu juice, a dusting of zest, and a single shiso flower for balance.

As you finish this small offering, notice how the bitterness of the charred rind lingers softly against the sweetness of the miso. It is a reminder that beauty often resides in the fleeting and the imperfect. May this citrus warmth stay with you throughout your day.

*Hiroshi*
