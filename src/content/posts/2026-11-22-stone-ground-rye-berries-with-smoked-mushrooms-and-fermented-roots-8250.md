## Introduction

In my Black Forest kitchen, the sound of the heavy granite mortar and pestle signals dinner time. Today we celebrate the humble root cellar with stone-ground toasted rye berries and roasted winter roots, tossed in a velvety, house-fermented garlic emulsion and crowned with wood-smoked woodland mushrooms. It is grounding, fragrant with beech smoke and thyme, and meant to be spooned generously straight from a heavy earthenware bowl for the whole family.

## Ingredients

* 250 g whole rye berries, soaked overnight
* 400 g mixed woodland mushrooms (chanterelles, king oysters, and chestnut mushrooms), torn
* 300 g parsnips, scrubbed and sliced into bite-sized wedges
* 250 g black salsify (Schwarzwurzeln), peeled and cut into batons
* 1 medium Savoy cabbage, quartered and sliced into thick ribbons
* 3 tbsp cold-pressed rapeseed oil
* 2 tbsp stone-ground coarse German mustard
* 4 cloves lacto-fermented garlic (or roasted garlic), mashed
* 2 tbsp dark malt beer or vegetable broth for deglazing
* 1 handful fresh fir needles or beechwood chips (for smoking)
* 1 bunch fresh thyme, leaves picked
* 1 tsp caraway seeds, lightly cracked in a mortar
* Coarse sea salt and cracked black pepper to taste
* 2 tbsp chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley for garnish

## Instructions

* Drain the soaked rye berries, bring to a gentle boil in salted water, and simmer until tender yet pleasantly chewy, roughly 40 minutes, then drain and crush roughly in a stone mortar to release their nutty starch.
* Heat a cast-iron pan over medium-high heat with beechwood chips or fir needles until lightly smoking; smoke the mushrooms in a perforated pan atop for 6 minutes, then sear quickly in 1 tablespoon of rapeseed oil until browned.
* In a wide heavy skillet, roast the parsnips and black salsify in rapeseed oil over medium heat until golden and tender, roughly 12 minutes.
* Add the Savoy cabbage, cracked caraway seeds, and thyme to the roots, sautéing until the cabbage edges wilt and char slightly.
* Deglaze the skillet with dark malt beer, stirring up all the caramelized bits from the bottom.
* Whisk the stone-ground coarse mustard and mashed fermented garlic into the skillet to form an unctuous, rich pan glaze.
* Fold the stone-crushed rye berries into the glazed vegetables, seasoning with coarse sea salt and cracked black pepper.
* Top the warm skillet mixture with the beech-smoked mushrooms, finish with fresh chopped parsley, and serve immediately family-style.

Set this dish in the center of a worn wooden board, pass around the rustic spoons, and let the earthy warmth settle in. Simple roots and ancient grains, when crushed with intention and wood-smoked patience, make for the most comforting family supper imaginable. Guten Appetit!
