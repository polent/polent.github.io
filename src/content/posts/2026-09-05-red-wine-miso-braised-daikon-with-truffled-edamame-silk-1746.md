## Introduction

As the frost settles on the temple gardens, the winter daikon reaches its peak sweetness. This dish is a quiet dialogue between the slow, patient braising techniques of the West and the deep umami of our house-aged red miso. It is a meditation on time, gravity, and the earth's quiet transition into winter.

## Ingredients

* 1 large winter daikon (approx 1kg), peeled and cut into 4cm rounds
* 500ml Kombu dashi (cold-extracted overnight)
* 200ml Vegan dry red wine (such as a Japanese Muscat Bailey A or Pinot Noir)
* 3 tbsp Aged red miso (Aka Miso)
* 2 tbsp Tamari (aged)
* 1 piece Dried kombu (5cm square)
* 200g Fresh edamame, shelled
* 1 tsp Black truffle oil or fresh truffle shavings
* 10cm Burdock root (gobo), shaved into thin ribbons
* Small handful of micro-shiso or seasonal purple flowers
* 1 tsp Organic coconut sugar

## Instructions

* Begin by beveling the edges of the daikon rounds (mentori) with a sharp knife to prevent them from breaking during the long simmer.
* Parboil the daikon in rice-wash water (togijiru) for 15 minutes to remove any bitterness, then rinse gently in cold water.
* In a heavy bottomed ceramic pot, combine the kombu dashi, red wine, tamari, and the piece of kombu. Place the daikon rounds in a single layer.
* Bring to a very faint simmer. Cover with an otoshibuta (drop lid) and cook on the lowest heat possible for 90 minutes. The daikon should become translucent and dark with the wine.
* In the final 15 minutes, whisk the red miso and coconut sugar with a little of the braising liquid until smooth, then stir it back into the pot. Let it glaze the radish slowly.
* While the daikon rests, steam the edamame for 5 minutes. Blend with a splash of dashi and truffle oil until it reaches a silken, airy consistency.
* Flash-fry the shaved burdock ribbons in a neutral oil until golden and crisp, mimicking the flavor of binchotan smoke.
* To plate, place a generous sweep of the green edamame silk on an asymmetric ceramic plate. Position one daikon round slightly off-center. Top with a nest of crispy burdock and a single shiso bud.

Eat with presence. Feel the warmth of the binchotan-kissed burdock and the depth of the fermented miso. In this bowl, the seasons meet and find stillness. May it nourish your spirit as much as your body.

*Hiroshi*
