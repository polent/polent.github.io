## Introduction

Benvenuti to my kitchen here in the hills. Today, I am taking a tradition from the south of Italy—the marinated insalata di rinforzo—and elevating it into a contemporary marinated cocktail. This dish celebrates the crunch of winter cauliflower from the orto, the brine of hand-picked capers, and the liquid gold of our first-press olive oil. It is a bright, acidic, and deeply textured start to any meal, proving that even the most humble vegetables can shine like jewels when treated with patience and respect.

## Ingredients

* 1 medium head of white cauliflower, broken into very small, bite-sized florets
* 1/2 cup Castelvetrano olives, pitted and halved
* 2 tablespoons salted capers, thoroughly rinsed and patted dry
* 1 large roasted red bell pepper, peeled and sliced into thin ribbons
* 1/4 cup high-quality red wine vinegar
* 1/2 cup extra virgin olive oil (new press if available)
* 1 teaspoon dried wild oregano
* 1/2 teaspoon red chili flakes (peperoncino)
* 2 tablespoons toasted pine nuts
* Fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
* Flaky sea salt to taste

## Instructions

* Quickly blanch the cauliflower florets in boiling salted water for exactly 2 minutes; they must remain crisp and firm (al dente), then immediately plunge them into an ice bath.
* In a large glass bowl, whisk together the red wine vinegar, extra virgin olive oil, dried oregano, and peperoncino until emulsified.
* Drain the cauliflower well and pat dry with a clean kitchen towel to ensure the marinade clings to the florets.
* Add the cauliflower, olives, capers, and roasted pepper ribbons to the bowl with the marinade.
* Toss everything gently by hand to ensure every crevice of the cauliflower is coated.
* Cover and let the vegetables marinate at room temperature for at least 2 hours, or in the refrigerator overnight for a deeper flavor profile.
* Just before serving, toss in the toasted pine nuts and fresh parsley for a burst of color and crunch.
* Spoon the marinated mixture into wide-rimmed cocktail glasses or small ceramic bowls, drizzling a little extra marinade over the top and finishing with a pinch of flaky sea salt.

This cocktail is best enjoyed after the flavors have had a long afternoon to get to know one another. Serve it in your finest glassware to honor the ingredients, alongside a thick slice of toasted pane sciocco. It is a reminder that the simplest gifts of the garden are often the most profound. A presto!
