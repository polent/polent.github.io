## Introduction

Habari! Today, I’m bringing the warmth of the Mombasa sun to your kitchen. This snack celebrates the humble cassava, transformed by the sizzle of a hot tava and the deep, complex heat of Ethiopian berbere. It is a bite of the coast and the highlands all in one, perfectly capturing the Indian Ocean breeze and the red soil of our home.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups grated fresh cassava (muhogo), moisture squeezed out
* 1/2 cup thick coconut milk, pressed fresh
* 1 tablespoon berbere spice blend
* 1 teaspoon toasted cumin seeds
* 2 green pilipili chiles, finely minced
* 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
* 1 tablespoon coconut sugar or maple syrup
* 1/2 cup roasted groundnuts, crushed for topping
* Fresh coriander and lime wedges for garnish
* Sea salt to taste
* Coconut oil for searing

## Instructions

* In a large bowl, combine the grated cassava with the coconut milk, minced chiles, cumin seeds, and a pinch of salt. Mix until a thick, moldable dough forms.
* Shape the mixture into small, flat round cakes, approximately 2 inches in diameter.
* In a small bowl, whisk together the tamarind paste, berbere spice, and coconut sugar to create a thick glaze.
* Heat a tava or heavy cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat and lightly coat with coconut oil.
* Place the cassava cakes on the skillet and sear for 4 to 5 minutes per side until a golden-brown, crispy crust develops.
* Brush the top of each cake generously with the berbere tamarind glaze and cook for one additional minute to allow it to caramelize.
* Remove from the heat and immediately sprinkle with crushed groundnuts and fresh coriander.

Serve these while the warmth of the pan still lingers. A squeeze of lime and a handful of fresh kachumbari on the side is all you need to transport yourself to the shores of Mombasa. Karibuni!
