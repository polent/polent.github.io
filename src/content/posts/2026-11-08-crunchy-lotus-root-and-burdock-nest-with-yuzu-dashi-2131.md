## Introduction

In the quiet temple kitchens of Kyoto, crunch is not mere loudness—it is an awakening of the senses. Inspired by festival street energy yet grounded in shojin ryori, this dish honors the autumn-to-winter root harvest. Paper-thin shaved burdock and lotus root are flash-crisped in light rice bran oil, showered with popped wild rice blossoms, and served over warm, fragrant yuzu-infused kombu dashi.

## Ingredients

- 1 medium fresh renkon (lotus root), peeled and sliced into paper-thin rounds
- 1 slender gobo (burdock root), thoroughly scrubbed and shaved into airy ribbons
- 3 tablespoons uncooked heirloom black rice
- 1/2 cup cold water
- 1/2 cup pure katakuriko (potato starch)
- 1 teaspoon toasted black sesame seeds
- High-smoke-point rice bran oil, for flash-frying
- 1 cup cold-brewed kombu dashi
- 1 tablespoon aged artisan tamari
- 1 tablespoon sweet mirin
- Zest of 1 fresh organic yuzu
- 1/2 teaspoon flaky sea salt blended with freshly ground green sansho pepper

## Instructions

- Soak shaved burdock ribbons and lotus root rounds in ice-cold water with a drop of rice vinegar for 10 minutes to retain maximum crispness and balance natural tannins, then drain and pat thoroughly dry on linen.
- Warm a dry cast-iron pan over medium-high heat and add the black rice, gently shaking until the grains pop into miniature, crackling crisp blossoms; set aside.
- Simmer the overnight kombu dashi, aged tamari, and mirin together in a small earthenware pot for 5 minutes, then finish with finely shaved yuzu zest and keep warm.
- Heat 5 centimeters of rice bran oil in a deep heavy pot to 175°C (345°F).
- Lightly toss dried roots in potato starch, shaking off excess so only an ethereal veil clings to each ribbon.
- Drop loosely gathered clusters of burdock and lotus root into the hot oil, flash-frying for 2 to 3 minutes until delicately golden and intensely crisp.
- Drain briefly on bamboo paper, dusting instantly with sansho salt, toasted sesame seeds, and popped black rice.
- Transfer to an asymmetrical dark stoneware bowl and serve alongside the warm yuzu dashi for immediate dipping.

Serve the nest while the oil crackle is still alive and the broth emits soft steam. Let each bite awaken the senses to the fleeting perfection of autumn roots and temple craft.

*Hiroshi*
