## Introduction

Hey there! This morning at the market, the nopales were perfectly firm and the heirloom beans looked like little gems. I wanted to create something that captures that al dente bite—something with texture, soul, and a bit of crunch. These tostaditas are a coastal California love letter: smoky from the almond wood grill, bright with Ojai citrus, and deeply satisfying. It is exactly the kind of unfussy, sun-drenched snack I love to share after a morning in the surf.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup dried heirloom pinto or Flor de Mayo beans, soaked overnight
- 2 large fresh nopal paddles, thorns removed and edges trimmed
- 1 cup sun-ripened cherry tomatoes, halved
- 2 ears of Central Valley sweet corn, husks removed
- 1/4 cup Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
- 2 tablespoons verjus or fresh lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon chipotle in adobo, finely minced
- 1 teaspoon preserved lemon peel, minced
- 12 small corn tortillas (hand-pressed masa if possible)
- A handful of fresh cilantro and opal basil leaves
- Flaky sea salt and smoked paprika to taste

## Instructions

- Simmer the soaked heirloom beans in salted water with a clove of garlic until they are tender but still offer a distinct bite, about 45 to 60 minutes. Drain and set aside to cool.
- Prepare your grill over almond wood or medium-high heat. Brush the corn and nopal paddles with a little olive oil.
- Grill the corn until charred in spots and the nopales until they turn olive green and have beautiful grill marks (about 4 minutes per side).
- Dice the grilled nopales into 1/2-inch cubes and shear the sweet corn kernels off the cob.
- In a large bowl, whisk together the Sonoma olive oil, verjus, minced chipotle, and preserved lemon.
- Fold in the al dente beans, grilled nopales, corn, and cherry tomatoes. Let the flavors marry for 10 minutes.
- Lightly brush the corn tortillas with oil and grill or toast them until crisp and golden.
- Spoon the vibrant bean and cactus mixture onto each crisp tortilla.
- Finish with a scatter of fresh herbs, a dusting of smoked paprika, and a final sprinkle of flaky sea salt.

I hope this brings a bit of that Golden State sunshine to your kitchen. Grab a glass of chilled verjus or a local pét-nat, find a sunlit spot on the porch, and enjoy every vibrant crunch. There is nothing like the snap of fresh produce to make the day feel special. Happy cooking!
