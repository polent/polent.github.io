## Introduction

In shojin ryori, we look to the charcoal flame not to conquer an ingredient, but to awaken its innate sweetness. Today, we take tender winter-sweetened daikon, gently simmered in aged kombu dashi until translucent, and glaze it over glowing binchotan coals with aged saikyo miso and fermented sansho pepper. It is warm, comforting, and a quiet meditation on seasonal depth.

## Ingredients

* 1 large seasonal daikon radish, peeled and sliced into 3 cm thick rounds
* 1 piece ma-kombu kelp (about 10 cm)
* 800 ml spring water
* 1 tablespoon washed uncooked rice (to temper the daikon's bitterness during blanching)
* 3 tablespoons Saikyo sweet white miso
* 1 tablespoon aged mirin
* 1 teaspoon tamari soy sauce
* 1/2 teaspoon ground fermented green sansho pepper
* 1 teaspoon toasted white sesame seeds
* Shiso leaf microgreens or edible kinome leaves for garnish

## Instructions

* Score both faces of each daikon round with a delicate shallow crosshatch pattern to welcome the dashi and heat.
* In a clay donabe or pot, combine spring water, kombu, uncooked rice, and the daikon. Bring to a bare simmer over low heat for 35 minutes until a bamboo skewer glides through like silk.
* Remove daikon rounds gently and pat dry on a linen towel. Let them breathe for 10 minutes.
* Whisk together Saikyo miso, aged mirin, tamari, and ground sansho pepper in a small bowl until lustrous and glossy.
* Prepare a small tabletop binchotan charcoal grill or cast-iron grill pan until radiant.
* Place daikon directly on the grill bars for 3 minutes per side until lightly charred and smoky.
* Generously brush the top face with the sweet miso glaze and grill for another 1 to 2 minutes until caramelized and fragrant with delicate blistered spots.
* Transfer onto an unglazed, rustic wabi-sabi ceramic dish. Finish with toasted white sesame seeds and a single kinome sprig.

Serve this dish immediately while the crust is crackling and the interior remains meltingly soft. Pair with a bowl of steamed newly-harvested rice and a quiet cup of roasted hojicha. May every bite offer a moment of stillness and gratitude for the earth's bounty.

*Hiroshi*
