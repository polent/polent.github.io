## Introduction

Greetings. I am Hiroshi. Today, we bridge the gap between the modern craving for indulgence and the ancient stillness of the temple. This pastry is a vessel for umami, using the natural sweetness of fermented rice and the velvety body of morning-made tofu. It is a study in texture—crisp, air-filled rice crust meeting a heart of fermented creaminess.

## Ingredients

- 100g Fine rice flour
- 50g Refined coconut oil
- 150ml Unsweetened organic soy milk
- 1 pinch Sea salt
- 1 tablespoon Flax meal mixed with 3 tablespoons warm water
- 200g Fresh silken tofu, pressed for 1 hour
- 2 tablespoons Aged white shiro miso
- 3 tablespoons Amazake (fermented rice sweetener)
- 1 teaspoon Pure vanilla bean paste
- 50ml Heavy coconut cream, chilled
- 6 Salted sakura blossoms, rinsed and dried
- 1 teaspoon Hojicha powder for dusting

## Instructions

- To make the cream, blend the pressed silken tofu, shiro miso, amazake, and vanilla in a high-speed blender until it reaches a velvet-like consistency.
- Whip the chilled coconut cream into soft peaks and gently fold it into the tofu mixture. Transfer to a piping bag and chill for at least 2 hours.
- Pre-heat your oven to 180C. In a small pot, bring the soy milk, coconut oil, and salt to a gentle boil.
- Remove from heat and stir in the rice flour quickly with a wooden spoon until a smooth dough ball forms and pulls away from the sides.
- Allow the dough to cool slightly, then gradually work in the flax-egg mixture until the dough is glossy and holds its shape.
- Pipe 6 round mounds onto a baking sheet lined with parchment paper.
- Bake for 25-30 minutes until the shells are golden and feel light. Do not open the oven door during the first 20 minutes.
- Once cooled completely, poke a small hole in the base of each puff and pipe in the chilled miso cream until full.
- Garnish the top with a single salted sakura blossom and a light sifting of hojicha powder.

Eat this in silence, perhaps with a bowl of thin matcha. Notice how the salt of the miso meets the floral ghost of the sakura. In this bite, the season turns. This is the essence of shun—honoring the fleeting moment when the air begins to warm.

*Hiroshi*
