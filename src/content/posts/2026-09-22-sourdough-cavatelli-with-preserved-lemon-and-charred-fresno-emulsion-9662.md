## Introduction

The fog is just lifting off the coast, and the morning air at the market was thick with the scent of sun-warmed herbs and salt spray. Today, I am leaning into that electric, mouth-watering brightness we are seeing everywhere right now—the sour pasta trend. My version is a love letter to the Central Coast, using sourdough pasta for a fermented tang, layered with the deep acid of preserved lemons and a splash of verjus from the valley. It is sharp, coastal, and perfectly unfussy.

## Ingredients

* 1 lb sourdough cavatelli or gemelli pasta
* 1/3 cup Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
* 2 Fresno chiles, charred over an open flame and finely diced
* 1/2 preserved lemon, rind only, rinsed and minced
* 3 cloves garlic, paper-thin shavings
* 1 cup Sun-Gold cherry tomatoes, kept whole
* 2 tbsp verjus or Meyer lemon juice
* 1 tsp fermented chile brine (from the jar)
* 1/4 cup toasted pepitas for crunch
* 1/2 cup fresh cilantro and mint, roughly torn
* Flaky sea salt and smoked adobo flakes to taste

## Instructions

* Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the sourdough pasta until it has a perfect al dente bite.
* While the pasta boils, char your Fresno chiles over a gas flame or under the broiler until the skins are blistered. Peel away the blackened bits, remove seeds if you prefer less heat, and finely dice.
* In a large, heavy-bottomed skillet, warm the olive oil over medium-low heat. Add the shaved garlic and cook until translucent and fragrant, being careful not to let it brown.
* Increase the heat to medium and toss in the Sun-Gold tomatoes. Sauté for 3-4 minutes until they just begin to burst, releasing their sweet nectar.
* Stir in the diced Fresno chiles, minced preserved lemon, verjus, and fermented chile brine. This is where that signature sour profile builds.
* Reserve about 1/2 cup of the starchy pasta water, then drain the pasta.
* Add the pasta directly into the skillet. Pour in the reserved water and toss vigorously over high heat for 60 seconds to create a glossy, acidic emulsion that coats every ridge of the pasta.
* Remove from heat and fold in the fresh herbs and toasted pepitas.
* Plate immediately with a final sprinkle of smoked adobo flakes and a drizzle of your best olive oil.

This dish is best enjoyed with your feet up and a glass of chilled, crisp Vermentino in hand. It captures that specific moment when the afternoon sun hits the patio just right. I hope it brings a bit of that California brightness to your kitchen today. Stay salty and stay hungry.

*Emily*
