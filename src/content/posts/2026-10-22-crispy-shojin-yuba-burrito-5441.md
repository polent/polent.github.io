## Introduction

A quiet bridge between Kyoto temple tradition and modern craveability. Wrapped in sheets of fresh yuba and pan-crisped to a golden shatter, this burrito holds dashi-simmered maitake mushrooms, nukazuke daikon for bright crunch, seasoned haiga-mai rice, and a velvety whipped silken tofu and sesame emulsion.

## Ingredients

* 4 large sheets fresh yuba (bean curd skin)
* 1 cup cooked warm haiga-mai (half-milled rice) or short-grain brown rice
* 150 g maitake mushrooms, pulled into bite-sized clusters
* 100 g firm cotton tofu (momen tofu), pressed and cut into strips
* 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil, divided
* 1 tbsp aged tamari
* 1 tbsp mirin
* 50 ml rich kombu dashi
* 30 g nukazuke pickled daikon, cut into fine julienne strips
* 4 fresh green shiso leaves
* 50 g silken tofu, blotted dry
* 1 tbsp white sesame paste (neri goma)
* 1 tsp rice vinegar
* 1/2 tsp sea salt
* 1 pinch freshly grated yuzu zest

## Instructions

* Whisk silken tofu, neri goma, rice vinegar, and a pinch of salt until velvety and smooth to create the sesame emulsion.
* Heat 1 tablespoon of sesame oil in a cast iron skillet over medium-high heat; sear maitake and pressed momen tofu until deeply browned on the edges.
* Glaze the seared mushrooms and tofu with kombu dashi, aged tamari, and mirin, letting the liquid reduce until the ingredients glisten and are fully coated.
* Lay two overlapping sheets of fresh yuba onto a flat clean surface; gently spread warm haiga-mai in the center, leaving borders clear.
* Layer shiso leaves, the glazed maitake and tofu, julienned nukazuke daikon, and a spoonful of sesame emulsion over the rice bed.
* Fold the sides of the yuba inward, then tightly roll from the bottom up into a compact, neat cylinder.
* Warm the remaining tablespoon of sesame oil in the pan over medium heat; place the burrito seam-side down and griddle gently for 3 to 4 minutes per side until the skin forms a blistered, golden-crisp shell.
* Slice diagonally with a single clean stroke of a sharp knife and garnish the cut surface with a delicate dusting of yuzu zest before serving.

Parting the crisp yuba wrapper reveals a warm balance of umami, earth, and brine. Serve immediately while the crust crackles softly, accompanied by a small cup of steeped hojicha or a single drop of aged tamari.

*Hiroshi*
