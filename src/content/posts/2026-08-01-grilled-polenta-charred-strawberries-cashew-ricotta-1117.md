## Introduction

Benvenuti to my terrace! Today, as the Tuscan sun begins to warm the stone walls of my hill town, we embrace the griglia for a brunch that honors the simple gifts of the garden. We are taking the humble polenta of our cucina povera tradition and kissing it with fire. This dish is a beautiful dance between the smoky char of the grill, the sweetness of the season's first strawberries, and the creamy richness of our plant-based ricotta. It is simple, it is honest, and it is exactly what a slow morning requires.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup coarse organic cornmeal (polenta)
* 3 cups filtered water
* 2 cups fresh seasonal strawberries, halved
* 1 cup raw cashews, soaked overnight and drained
* 2 tablespoons aged balsamic vinegar of Modena
* 4 tablespoons cold-press extra virgin olive oil
* 1 organic lemon, zested and juiced
* A handful of fresh mint and basil leaves
* Fine sea salt and cracked black pepper

## Instructions

* Bring the water and a pinch of salt to a boil in a heavy-bottomed pot. Slowly whisk in the polenta and simmer on low heat, stirring often, for about 30 minutes until thick and creamy.
* Pour the polenta into a lightly oiled square tray, smoothing the top. Allow it to cool and set in the fridge for at least 2 hours until firm.
* For the ricotta, blend the soaked cashews, lemon juice, half the lemon zest, a pinch of salt, and a splash of water until creamy but slightly textured. Fold in finely chopped mint.
* Slice the firm polenta into thick triangles. Brush each side generously with extra virgin olive oil.
* Toss the halved strawberries in a bowl with the balsamic vinegar and a tiny drizzle of olive oil.
* Heat your grill to medium-high. Place the polenta triangles on the grates and grill for 4-5 minutes per side until deep golden grill marks appear and the edges are crisp.
* Use a grill basket or skewers to quickly char the strawberries for 2 minutes, just until they begin to soften and release their juices.
* To assemble, spread a thick layer of cashew ricotta over the warm polenta slabs. Top with the charred strawberries, a drizzle of the remaining balsamic from the bowl, fresh basil leaves, and a final flourish of new-press olive oil.

Serve these warm while the polenta is still crisp from the flames. I love to pair this with a small glass of chilled sparkling water or a strong, dark espresso. It is a dish that proves you do not need much to feast like royalty—only fire, a few perfect ingredients, and the patience to let them shine. Buon appetito!

*Isabella*
