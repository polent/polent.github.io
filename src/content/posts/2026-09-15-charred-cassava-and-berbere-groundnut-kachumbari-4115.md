## Introduction

Karibu! Today, I am bringing the warmth of my charcoal jiko to your table. This side dish is a love letter to the red soil of the highlands and the salty breeze of Mombasa—crunchy, tangy, and kissed by the fire. It is an experimental twist on the classic kachumbari, elevated with the smokiness of charred cassava and the heat of the Horn of Africa.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups fresh cassava, peeled, boiled until tender, and cut into bite-sized cubes
* 1/2 cup roasted groundnuts (peanuts)
* 1 tablespoon berbere spice blend
* 2 large Roma tomatoes, finely diced
* 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced and soaked in salt water for 5 minutes
* 1-2 fresh pilipili chiles (or bird’s eye chiles), minced
* A handful of fresh cilantro (dhania), chopped
* Juice of 2 fresh limes
* 1/4 cup pomegranate seeds for a modern floral pop
* 1 tablespoon cold-pressed coconut oil
* Sea salt to taste

## Instructions

* Toss the roasted groundnuts with half the berbere spice and a tiny drop of coconut oil in a warm pan over low heat until fragrant and well-coated. Set aside to cool.
* Heat a cast-iron tava or heavy skillet over high heat. Lightly coat the boiled cassava cubes with the remaining berbere and a touch of coconut oil.
* Place the cassava in the hot pan, charring them until the edges are golden-brown and crispy, evoking the smoky essence of a coastal charcoal jiko.
* In a medium ceramic bowl, combine the diced tomatoes, drained red onions, minced pilipili, and chopped cilantro.
* Gently fold the warm, charred cassava into the fresh tomato and onion mixture.
* Squeeze the fresh lime juice over the entire dish and season with sea salt to bring out the brightness.
* Just before serving, garnish generously with the spiced groundnuts and pomegranate seeds to ensure the textures remain distinct and crunchy.

Serve this alongside a steaming bowl of njahi stew or some fresh, flaky chapatis. It is the crunch and the zing that makes the meal complete. I hope every bite brings a bit of the Indian Ocean sun into your kitchen. Enjoy your meal, or as we say, Karibuni tule!

*Nia*
