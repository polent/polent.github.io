## Introduction

Comfort food in my coastal kitchen always starts with a low oven and a generous pour of Sonoma olive oil. This slow-confit heirloom dish brings together sun-ripened market sungolds, sweet melting shallots, and buttery heirloom beans, all kissed by California citrus and subtle chile warmth. It is pure, grounding market comfort designed for dipping warm bread or scooping with fresh charred tortillas.

## Ingredients

- 1.5 cups California extra virgin olive oil
- 1 pint mixed heirloom cherry tomatoes (Sungold and Early Girl varieties)
- 2 cups cooked large heirloom white beans (such as Royal Corona or Ayocote Blanco), rinsed and drained
- 8 cloves California garlic, peeled and gently smashed
- 3 medium shallots, peeled and halved lengthwise
- 1 fresh red Fresno chile, thinly sliced
- 1 dried guajillo chile, stemmed and seeded
- 4 sprigs fresh lemon thyme
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 1 tbsp finely chopped preserved lemon peel
- 1 Meyer lemon, halved for charring
- 1/4 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley and cilantro, roughly torn
- 1 tsp flaky Pacific sea salt
- Fresh cracked black pepper to taste
- Warm grilled sourdough or fresh masa flatbreads, for serving

## Instructions

- Preheat your oven to 300°F (150°C).
- In a shallow ceramic baking dish or cast-iron cazuela, arrange the cherry tomatoes, smashed garlic cloves, halved shallots, sliced Fresno chile, dried guajillo chile, and herb sprigs.
- Pour the olive oil over the aromatics, ensuring everything is submerged or well-coated.
- Transfer to the oven and slow-roast uncovered for 45 to 55 minutes, until the tomatoes have blistered and collapsed, the garlic is meltingly tender, and the oil is deeply aromatic.
- Carefully fold the cooked heirloom beans into the baking dish, gently coating them in the warm oil, and return to the oven for 10 minutes to heat through.
- While the confit finishes, heat a dry skillet over high heat and sear the Meyer lemon halves cut-side down until caramelized and deeply charred, about 3 minutes.
- Remove the confit from the oven, discard the woody herb stems, and scatter the finely chopped preserved lemon and fresh torn herbs across the top.
- Squeeze the juice from the charred Meyer lemon over the dish, finish with flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper, and serve immediately with warm bread.

Set the warm earthenware pot right in the middle of the table, crack open a crisp coastal white or sparkling cider, and let everyone dive in. The luscious, tomato-infused dipping oil that pools at the bottom is pure liquid gold—do not let a single drop go to waste.
