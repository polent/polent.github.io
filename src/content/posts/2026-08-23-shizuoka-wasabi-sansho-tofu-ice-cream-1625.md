## Introduction

Welcome. In the quiet of the morning, I find that even the coldest elements can carry a hidden fire. Today, we explore the concept of piquant cooling. This ice cream is not a confection of sugar, but a meditation on the sharp, clean heat of Shizuoka wasabi and the electric tingle of sansho pepper, balanced by the gentle creaminess of my morning silken tofu. It is a dish that honors the transition of seasons—where the lingering chill of winter meets the spicy awakening of spring growth.

## Ingredients

* 300g fresh silken tofu, pressed lightly to remove excess water
* 200ml organic soy cream or full-fat coconut milk
* 100ml amazake (sweet fermented rice liquid)
* 40g organic cane sugar or light agave
* 2 tbsp freshly grated Shizuoka wasabi (grated in circles on an oroshigane)
* 1 tsp ground green sansho pepper
* 1/4 tsp sea salt
* 1 sheet toasted nori, crumbled into fine dust
* Fresh kinome leaves or shiso flowers for garnish

## Instructions

* Begin by passing the silken tofu through a fine mesh sieve or tamago-koshiki to ensure a texture as smooth as silk.
* In a chilled bowl, whisk the soy cream with the sugar and sea salt until the sugar has completely dissolved and the mixture is slightly aerated.
* Fold the sieved tofu and the amazake into the cream mixture with a bamboo spatula, moving with intention and care.
* Gently incorporate the freshly grated wasabi and the sansho pepper. The fragrance should be sharp and citrusy.
* Pour the mixture into an ice cream maker and churn until it reaches the consistency of soft-peak snow.
* While the ice cream sets in the freezer for one hour, toast the nori sheet over a low flame until crisp, then grind it into a fine soil using a suribachi (mortar).
* To serve, place a single, modest scoop into an asymmetrical stoneware bowl. Sprinkle the nori soil on one side and crown with a single kinome leaf.

As you finish, you will notice the heat does not burn; it glows. The umami of the nori and the sting of the wasabi fade into a clean, sweet memory. I hope this small bowl brings you a moment of stillness in your busy day.

*Hiroshi*
