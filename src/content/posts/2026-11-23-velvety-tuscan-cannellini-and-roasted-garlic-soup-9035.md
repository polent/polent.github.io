## Introduction

In my Tuscan kitchen, winter cooking is all about embracing simplicity and warmth through the cucina povera philosophy. Today, I am sharing a velvety, velvety-smooth crema di cannellini and roasted garlic, enriched with slow-simmered cavolo nero and finished with a fragrant rosemary-infused olio nuovo. It is comforting, deeply nourishing, and honors the garden's finest seasonal offerings.

## Ingredients

* 2 cans (400g each) cannellini beans, rinsed and drained (or 500g cooked dried beans)
* 1 whole head of garlic
* 1 large yellow onion, finely diced
* 1 stalk celery, finely chopped
* 1 medium carrot, finely chopped
* 4 cups rich vegetable broth
* 1 bunch cavolo nero (lacinato kale), stems removed and leaves shredded
* 1 sprig fresh rosemary
* 2 fresh sage leaves
* 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil, plus extra for drizzling
* 1 tablespoon nutritional yeast for depth
* Sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). Slice the top off the head of garlic to expose the cloves, drizzle with olive oil, wrap in parchment and foil, and roast for 35 to 40 minutes until completely tender and caramelized.
* In a heavy-bottomed terracotta pot or Dutch oven, warm 2 tablespoons of olive oil over medium heat. Add the diced onion, celery, and carrot with a pinch of sea salt, sweating gently for 10 minutes until soft and translucent.
* Squeeze the roasted garlic cloves from their skins into the pot, stirring well to melt them into the soffritto.
* Add the cannellini beans, vegetable broth, rosemary sprig, and sage leaves. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower the heat, cover, and let simmer for 20 minutes to meld the flavors.
* Remove the rosemary and sage. Using an immersion blender, blend the soup until completely silky, creamy, and smooth. Stir in the nutritional yeast and season with salt and cracked black pepper.
* In a small separate skillet, flash-sauté the shredded cavolo nero in 1 tablespoon of olive oil for 2 minutes until tender but bright green.
* Ladle the soft cream into warm ceramic bowls, nestle the sautéed cavolo nero on top, and finish with a generous drizzle of green, cold-pressed olive oil.

Serve this velvety soup piping hot with thick slices of wood-fired sourdough rubbed with raw garlic. A generous final swirl of peppery extra virgin olive oil binds the entire bowl together. Buon appetito from my Tuscan table to yours!
