## Introduction

Jambo! Today, the air in Mombasa is thick with the scent of rain and sea salt, calling for something that warms the soul. I have crafted a side dish that bridges the gap between the sugary sweetness of our street mahamri and the earthy depth of the Kenyan highlands. These sweet potatoes are slow-simmered in a rich, syrupy infusion of tamarind and coastal spices until they glisten like jewels under the Indian Ocean sun.

## Ingredients

* 2 large red-skinned sweet potatoes, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
* 1 cup fresh coconut milk, first press
* 1/2 cup tamarind water, strained from pulp
* 1/4 cup jaggery or dark coconut sugar
* 1 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger
* 1/4 teaspoon sea salt
* 1/4 cup roasted groundnuts, roughly crushed
* Fresh mint leaves for garnish

## Instructions

* Place the sweet potato cubes into a heavy clay pot or a deep skillet.
* In a separate bowl, whisk together the coconut milk, tamarind water, jaggery, cardamom, ginger, and sea salt until the sugar is dissolved.
* Pour the spiced coconut mixture over the potatoes and place the pot over a medium-low flame.
* Simmer gently for about 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally to ensure even cooking.
* Continue cooking until the liquid has reduced into a thick, glossy syrup that clings to the potatoes and the tubers are tender to the fork.
* Remove from heat and transfer to a shallow serving bowl.
* Sprinkle generously with the crushed roasted groundnuts and garnish with a few torn mint leaves before serving.

Serve this while the syrup is still tacky and warm, letting the cardamom aroma fill your kitchen. It is the perfect companion to a spicy njahi bean stew or simply enjoyed on its own as the sun dips below the horizon. Karibuni chakula!

*Nia*
