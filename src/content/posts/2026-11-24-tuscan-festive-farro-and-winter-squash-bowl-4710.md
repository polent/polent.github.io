## Introduction

In Tuscany, winter celebrations are shaped by the fireplace, cold-pressed olio nuovo, and whatever bounty the orto offers. This festive grain bowl honors ancient farro dicocco layered with balsamic-glazed roasted winter squash, deeply crisped cavolo nero, and creamy cannellini beans tossed with crushed roasted chestnuts and ruby pomegranate pearls.

## Ingredients

* 1.5 cups semi-pearled Tuscan farro dicocco, thoroughly rinsed
* 3 cups vegetable broth infused with fresh rosemary and bay leaf
* 1 small kabocha or butternut squash, peeled, deseeded, and diced into bite-sized cubes
* 1.5 cups cooked cannellini beans, rinsed and drained
* 1 bunch Tuscan cavolo nero (lacinato kale), stems discarded and leaves roughly torn
* 1 cup cooked chestnuts, roughly chopped
* 1/2 cup fresh pomegranate arils
* 3 tablespoons aged Modena balsamic vinegar
* 1/4 cup top-tier extra virgin olive oil, plus extra for drizzling
* 2 cloves garlic, finely slivered
* 1 organic lemon, finely zested
* Flaky sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste

## Instructions

* Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F) and line a heavy baking sheet with parchment paper.
* Toss the diced squash with two tablespoons of extra virgin olive oil, balsamic vinegar, flaky sea salt, and black pepper, spreading evenly across the baking sheet.
* Roast the squash for 25 to 30 minutes, turning once halfway through, until edges are caramelized and tender.
* Simmer the rinsed farro in the herb-infused vegetable broth over medium-low heat for 20 to 25 minutes until al dente, drain excess liquid, and fluff gently.
* Heat a generous splash of olive oil in a wide iron skillet over medium heat, add the slivered garlic, then fold in the cavolo nero and sauté until wilted and slightly crisp along the borders.
* Gently warm the cannellini beans and chopped chestnuts alongside the cavolo nero for the final two minutes to let the rustic aromas meld.
* Assemble the festive bowl by laying a bed of warm farro, artfully topping with sections of caramelized squash, garlicky cavolo nero, and the bean-chestnut medley.
* Finish with vibrant pomegranate arils, fresh lemon zest, flaky sea salt, and a vigorous ribbon of peppery cold-pressed olive oil just before serving.

Serve this dish steaming warm straight from earthenware bowls, generously crowned with fresh zest and your greenest extra virgin olive oil. Buon appetito and happy holidays from my Tuscan kitchen to yours.
