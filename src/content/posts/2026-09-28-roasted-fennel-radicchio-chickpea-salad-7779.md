## Introduction

Benvenuti! In my garden high above the Tuscan mist, the transition of the seasons is written in the soil. Today, I am bringing together the crunch of the earth and the kiss of the fire. This roasted salad is a tribute to the cucina povera tradition—using yesterday’s sourdough, the last of the spring fennel, and the bold bitterness of radicchio. When we roast these ingredients, they transform: the fennel turns sweet like honey, and the bread becomes a golden vessel for our liquid gold olive oil. It is a warm, generous plate that speaks of the hillside and the hearth.

## Ingredients

* 2 medium fennel bulbs, sliced into 1cm wedges
* 1 large head of radicchio, sliced into thick ribbons
* 400g cooked chickpeas, patted completely dry
* 2 cups stale sourdough bread, torn into rustic bite-sized chunks
* 5 tablespoons premium extra virgin olive oil
* 1 organic lemon, zest and juice
* 1 tablespoon salt-packed capers, rinsed and chopped
* 60g toasted hazelnuts, roughly crushed
* A handful of fresh flat-leaf parsley, chopped
* Flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F) and prepare a large, shallow baking tray.
* Place the fennel wedges and dried chickpeas on the tray, drizzling with two tablespoons of olive oil and a generous pinch of salt. Toss well to coat.
* Roast for 15 minutes until the fennel begins to soften and the chickpeas start to crisp.
* Add the torn sourdough pieces and the radicchio ribbons to the tray. Drizzle with one more tablespoon of olive oil and toss everything together.
* Return to the oven for another 10 to 12 minutes, or until the bread is deeply golden and the edges of the radicchio are charred and crisp.
* While the salad roasts, whisk together the remaining olive oil, lemon juice, lemon zest, and chopped capers in a small jar.
* Transfer the warm roasted ingredients into a large wooden serving bowl.
* Pour the lemon-caper dressing over the salad while it is still hot, allowing the bread to soak up the bright flavors.
* Fold in the crushed hazelnuts and fresh parsley gently.
* Season with a final touch of flaky salt and black pepper before serving immediately.

Serve this while the vegetables still carry the hum of the oven's heat. It is a dish that needs nothing more than a quiet table and a glass of chilled Vermentino. Remember, the secret is not in the complexity, but in the quality of your oil and the char on your greens. Buon appetito!

*Isabella*
