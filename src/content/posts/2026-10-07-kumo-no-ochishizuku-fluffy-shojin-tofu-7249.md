## Introduction

Welcome to my kitchen. Today, we celebrate the lightness of being. This dish, a meditation on the mountain yam and the humble bean, seeks to capture the texture of the morning mist over the Kamo River. It is my contribution to the festival of fluff, where we find strength in softness. By whipping mountain yam into our handmade tofu, we create an ethereal texture that defies gravity, anchored only by the deep umami of aged tamari and kombu.

## Ingredients

- 400g fresh silken tofu, pressed for 2 hours
- 150g Nagaimo (Japanese mountain yam), peeled
- 500ml Kombu dashi (cold-extracted for 12 hours)
- 2 tablespoons aged tamari
- 1 tablespoon mirin (organic, sugar-free)
- 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, finely grated
- 2 tablespoons Katakuriko (potato starch)
- 3 stalks Mitsuba (Japanese parsley), chopped
- Rice bran oil for gentle frying
- A pinch of sea salt

## Instructions

- Gently press the silken tofu to remove excess moisture without bruising its delicate structure.
- Grate the Nagaimo into a bowl using a ceramic grater until it becomes a frothy, viscous foam. This is the secret to the fluffiness.
- In a mortar, mash the pressed tofu and fold it into the grated Nagaimo. Add a pinch of sea salt and whisk with chopsticks until the mixture is light and airy.
- Heat the rice bran oil to 170 degrees Celsius. Form the tofu mixture into small, cloud-like rounds and dust them very lightly with Katakuriko.
- Carefully drop the clouds into the oil. Fry until the exterior forms a thin, crystalline crust while the inside remains molten and soft. Drain on a bamboo mat.
- In a small pot, bring the Kombu dashi, tamari, and mirin to a simmer. This is your golden pool.
- Place one tofu cloud into a shallow, imperfect ceramic bowl. Pour the warm dashi around it, not over it, to preserve the crispness.
- Top with a small mound of grated ginger and a few leaves of mitsuba. Serve immediately while the steam still whispers.

Eat slowly. Feel the transition from the crisp exterior to the ethereal center. In this fleeting texture, we find the truth of the season. May it bring you a moment of quiet peace and a gentle heart.

*Hiroshi*
