## Introduction

Karibu! Today's sticky dinner leans into the lush sweetness of Swahili coconut treacle and the sharp tang of coastal tamarind. We glaze slow-roasted matoke green bananas and charred cassava chunks in a spicy-sweet jiko-inspired reduction loaded with crushed cardamom, roasted groundnuts, and fiery pilipili chiles, served bubbling over warm coconut rice.

## Ingredients

* 4 green cooking bananas (matoke), peeled and cut into thick chunks
* 1 medium fresh cassava root, peeled, boiled until tender, and chopped into bite-sized wedges
* 3 tablespoons cold-pressed coconut oil
* 4 tablespoons fresh tamarind paste
* 1/3 cup dark coconut sugar or jaggery syrup
* 1/2 cup fresh pressed thick coconut milk
* 2 fresh red pilipili (bird\'s eye) chiles, finely minced
* 1 teaspoon freshly ground green cardamom pods
* 1 teaspoon toasted cumin seeds, lightly cracked
* 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds, pulverized
* 3 cloves garlic, grated
* 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated
* 1/3 cup roasted Kenyan red-skin groundnuts, roughly crushed
* 1 lime, juiced
* 1/2 cup fresh kachumbari (diced tomato, red onion, cilantro, and sea salt) for serving
* Flaky sea salt to taste

## Instructions

* Steam or parboil the matoke chunks in salted water for about 8 minutes until just tender but holding their shape, then drain thoroughly alongside the boiled cassava wedges.
* Heat the coconut oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed clay pot or cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Add the matoke and cassava, searing for 5 to 7 minutes until crisp, golden, and charred along the edges. Remove and set aside.
* Lower the heat and add the minced garlic, ginger, crushed cardamom, cumin, fenugreek, and pilipili to the residual oil. Sauté for 60 seconds until intensely aromatic.
* Pour in the tamarind paste, coconut sugar syrup, and fresh coconut milk, stirring constantly as the mixture bubbles and reduces into a thick, glossy, sticky glaze.
* Return the charred matoke and cassava to the pot, tossing gently to coat every surface in the bubbling lacquer. Allow the pieces to caramelize for another 3 to 4 minutes.
* Squeeze fresh lime juice over the pan and toss in the crushed roasted groundnuts for texture.
* Remove from heat and serve immediately in shallow clay bowls, topped with fresh kachumbari relish.

Garnish with a handful of fresh kachumbari for that bright, acidic crunch against the caramelized molasses glaze. Tear off a piece of flaky tava-charred chapati to scoop up every last sticky drop. Until tomorrow, keep your fire burning low and fragrant.
