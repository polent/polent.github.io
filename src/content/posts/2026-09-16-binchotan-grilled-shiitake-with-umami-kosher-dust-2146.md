## Introduction

Welcome to my kitchen. Today, we listen to the forest. As the mountain air warms, the shiitake mushrooms reach their peak of meatiness. I have prepared a dish that honors the shun of these fungi, lightly kissed by the white heat of binchotan charcoal and finished with a delicate dusting of house-cured umami salt. It is a quiet meditation on earth and fire, a shojin ryori interpretation of what you might call dusted kosher—a purity of seasoning that elevates the essence of the ingredient.

## Ingredients

* 4 large fresh Donko shiitake mushrooms, thick-capped
* 1 piece (5cm) dried kombu, toasted until crisp
* 1 tablespoon high-quality flakey kosher salt
* 1 teaspoon dehydrated white miso powder
* 2 tablespoons cold-pressed sesame oil
* 1 teaspoon aged tamari
* A few sprigs of young shiso for garnish

## Instructions

* Gently wipe the shiitake mushrooms with a damp cloth to remove any forest debris; do not wash them in water.
* Trim the stems flush to the cap, scoring the tops of the mushrooms lightly in a diamond katsuramuki pattern to allow the heat to penetrate evenly.
* In a small ceramic mortar and pestle, grind the toasted kombu, miso powder, and flakey kosher salt together until it becomes a fine, ethereal dust.
* Whisk the sesame oil and tamari together in a small bowl and lightly brush the mushroom caps.
* Prepare the binchotan charcoal until it glows white with a steady, flameless heat.
* Place the mushrooms on the grill, gill-side down first, for 2 minutes to seal the moisture.
* Flip the mushrooms and grill until the natural juices begin to pool in the center of the upturned caps and the edges are slightly charred.
* Remove from the heat and immediately sprinkle the prepared umami kosher dust over the mushrooms using a fine sieve.
* Arrange on an asymmetric ceramic plate and garnish with a single leaf of torn shiso to honor wabi-sabi.

Serve this while the smoke still lingers in the gills of the mushroom. Each bite is a reminder of the forest floor and the purity of the season. The dust should feel like a light frost on a spring morning. I hope it brings a moment of stillness to your day. Enjoy the simplicity of being.

*Hiroshi*
