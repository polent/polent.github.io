## Introduction

There is a specific kind of magic that happens when the late afternoon sun hits a table in the Bay Area, and this baked meze is my tribute to those golden hours. It’s a dish that bridges the gap between a rugged coastal fire and a refined wine-country kitchen. By combining the earthy, grounding texture of heirloom beans with the bright, citrusy lift of Ojai lemons and the smoky depth of Central Valley chiles, we create something that feels both deeply rooted and effortlessly light. This isn't just a snack; it's a celebration of the soil and the sea, designed to be shared with friends while the salt air drifts through the window.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups cooked heirloom pinto beans, drained
* 1 cup organic masa harina
* 1/2 cup warm filtered water
* 1/4 cup premium Sonoma olive oil
* 1 cup heirloom cherry tomatoes, halved
* 2 tablespoons chipotle in adobo, finely minced
* 1 teaspoon flaky sea salt
* Zest and juice of 1 Ojai lemon
* 1/4 cup toasted pepitas (pumpkin seeds)
* 1/2 cup vegan almond-based feta, crumbled
* 1/4 cup fresh cilantro leaves, torn
* 1/4 cup fresh mint leaves, torn
* 1 teaspoon cracked black pepper

## Instructions

* Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). If you have access to a wood-fired oven, use almond wood for a sweet, nutty smoke.
* In a small mixing bowl, combine the masa harina with a pinch of salt and the warm water. Knead gently until a smooth dough forms. Roll the dough into small, marble-sized balls and flatten them slightly with your thumb to create small dimples.
* In a shallow ceramic baking dish, toss the pinto beans with the Sonoma olive oil, minced chipotle in adobo, and sea salt until evenly coated.
* Nestle the prepared masa disks and the halved cherry tomatoes into the bean mixture, ensuring some of the beans are still peeking through.
* Place the dish in the oven and bake for 22 to 25 minutes. You are looking for the tomatoes to burst and caramelize, and the masa disks to become golden and crisp around the edges.
* Remove from the oven and immediately zest the Ojai lemon over the top while it’s still sizzling.
* Sprinkle with the crumbled vegan feta, toasted pepitas, and cracked black pepper.
* Finish the dish by showering it with fresh cilantro and mint, and a final generous drizzle of olive oil.
* Serve warm directly from the dish with charred corn tortillas or simply a spoon.

I hope this dish brings a little bit of that California sunshine to your table today. Whether you are dipping a warm tortilla into the smoky beans or savoring a crisp masa dumpling, remember that the best food is always a reflection of the landscape and the people we share it with. Keep it simple, keep it seasonal, and always use the best olive oil you can find. Happy cooking!

*Emily*
