## Introduction

In temple cooking, breakfast is a quiet awakening of the senses. Drawing from the modern desire for vitality, this robust morning bowl transforms the Western smoothie bowl through the lens of shojin ryori. Rich roasted kabocha squash, velvety house-made silken tofu, and naturally sweet amazake form a dense, golden cream, crowned with the textural contrast of puffed buckwheat, toasted kuromame, and crisp persimmon ribbons.

## Ingredients

* 180 g kabocha squash, steamed until tender and chilled
* 120 g fresh silken tofu, lightly drained
* 100 ml non-alcoholic rice amazake
* 60 ml unsweetened soy milk or organic oat milk
* 1 tsp white sweet miso (saikyo miso)
* 1/2 tsp fresh ginger juice, finely grated and pressed
* 2 tbsp puffed soba (buckwheat groats), dry-toasted
* 1 tbsp roasted black soybeans (kuromame)
* 1/2 ripe fuyu persimmon, shaved into translucent ribbons using katsuramuki technique
* 1 tsp toasted white sesame seeds
* 3 small micro shiso leaves or edible sansho blossoms for garnish

## Instructions

* Place the chilled steamed kabocha, fresh silken tofu, sweet amazake, soy milk, saikyo miso, and fresh ginger juice into a high-powered blender.
* Blend on high until completely silky, thick, and uniform, stopping to tamp down if necessary to ensure a dense, spoonable consistency.
* Pour the rich golden base into a wide, hand-thrown ceramic bowl with an asymmetrical wabi-sabi finish.
* Arrange the toppings in a deliberate, uncluttered arc across the surface: place the folded ribbons of fuyu persimmon, cluster the puffed soba alongside the roasted kuromame, and sprinkle lightly with toasted sesame seeds.
* Finish by floating delicate micro shiso leaves over the persimmon, and serve immediately in mindful stillness.

Sit with the bowl cupped in both hands before taking your first spoon. Notice the balance of earth, fermented sweetness, and gentle crunch—a vibrant meditation crafted to nourish your day in harmony with nature.

*Hiroshi*
