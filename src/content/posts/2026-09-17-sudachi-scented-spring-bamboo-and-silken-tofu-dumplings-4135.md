## Introduction

In the quiet of the Kyoto morning, as the mist clings to the bamboo groves, I look for the first signs of the spring thaw. This dish is a bridge between the intricate art of Chinese dim sum and the minimalist discipline of Shojin Ryori. We use the 'shun' of early spring—tender bamboo shoots and the bright, zesty fragrance of sudachi citrus—to create a bite that is both grounding and uplifting. It is a meditation on texture and the fleeting nature of the season.

## Ingredients

* 12 translucent starch dumpling wrappers (wheat and tapioca starch mix)
* 150g fresh bamboo shoots (takenoko), pre-boiled in rice bran to remove bitterness
* 100g firm silken tofu, pressed for 30 minutes to remove excess moisture
* 2 dried shiitake mushrooms, rehydrated in kombu dashi and finely minced
* 1 teaspoon fresh sudachi zest (or yuzu as an alternative)
* 1 tablespoon aged tamari
* 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
* 1/2 teaspoon freshly grated ginger
* A pinch of sea salt
* Shiso leaves for lining the steamer
* For the dipping broth: 50ml kombu dashi, 1 teaspoon sudachi juice, and a drop of white miso

## Instructions

* Finely mince the pre-boiled bamboo shoots and the rehydrated shiitake mushrooms into a uniform brunoise.
* In a cedar bowl, gently crumble the pressed silken tofu and fold in the bamboo, shiitake, sudachi zest, ginger, tamari, and sesame oil. Mix with light hands to maintain the tofu's integrity.
* Place a small spoonful of the filling in the center of a starch wrapper. Moisten the edges with a touch of kombu dashi.
* Pleat the edges precisely, creating a crescent shape with 8 to 10 small folds, ensuring the seal is tight yet delicate.
* Prepare a bamboo steamer by lining it with fresh shiso leaves to prevent sticking and add a subtle herbal aroma.
* Arrange the dumplings in the steamer, leaving space between each for the breath of the steam.
* Steam over boiling water for exactly 6 minutes until the wrappers become translucent and the filling is heated through.
* Whisk the dipping broth ingredients together in a small, weathered ceramic bowl.
* Serve immediately, garnishing each dumpling with a singular, micro-thin sliver of sudachi peel for an extra burst of zest.

As you lift each dumpling, notice the scent of the citrus oil hitting the steam. This is the essence of 'zesty'—not a loud shout, but a bright, clear bell ringing in the mountain air. May this simple offering bring you peace and the nourishment of the changing seasons.

*Hiroshi*
