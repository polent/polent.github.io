## Introduction

Hey there, friend! Today we are leaning into that incredible chewy texture that makes plant-based cooking so satisfying. I found some gorgeous King Oyster mushrooms at the market this morning, and they are the perfect canvas for a deep, smoky marinade inspired by the Central Valley. By scoring the mushrooms and searing them slow under a heavy press, we create a concentrated, bouncy bite that feels just like a sun-drenched afternoon in a coastal surf-town kitchen. Let's get grilling!

## Ingredients

* 1 lb King Oyster mushrooms, sliced into 1/2-inch thick planks
* 3 tbsp chipotle peppers in adobo sauce, finely minced
* 1/4 cup fresh Ojai orange juice
* 2 tbsp Sonoma extra-virgin olive oil
* 1 tbsp agave nectar or maple syrup
* 2 cloves garlic, microplaned
* 1 tsp dried Mexican oregano, crushed between palms
* 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
* Flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper
* Fresh cilantro and lime wedges for serving
* Warm heirloom masa tortillas

## Instructions

* Use a sharp knife to score a crosshatch pattern into both sides of the mushroom planks, being careful not to cut all the way through. This allows the chewy marinade to penetrate deep into the fibers.
* In a wide shallow bowl, whisk together the minced chipotle, orange juice, olive oil, agave, garlic, oregano, and smoked paprika until a thick glaze forms.
* Toss the mushroom planks in the marinade, ensuring every crevice is coated. Let them marinate at room temperature for at least 30 minutes.
* Heat a heavy cast-iron skillet or a grill over medium-high heat (ideally over almond wood for that authentic NorCal aroma).
* Place the mushrooms in the hot pan. Use a second heavy skillet or a grill press to weigh them down. This compression is key to achieving that signature chewy, condensed texture.
* Sear for 5 minutes per side, or until the edges are charred and the marinade has caramelized into a sticky lacquer.
* During the last minute of cooking, brush any remaining marinade over the top and let it bubble.
* Transfer to a wooden board, sprinkle with flaky sea salt, and serve immediately with warm tortillas and a bright squeeze of lime.

I love serving these right off the cast iron while they are still sticky and hot. Pair them with a crisp glass of Sonoma white wine or a chilled verjus sparkler. It is unfussy, coastal, and exactly what a summer lunch should be. Enjoy the chew!

*Emily*
