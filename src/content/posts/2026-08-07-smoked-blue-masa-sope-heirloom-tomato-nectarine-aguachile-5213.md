## Introduction

Today’s menu is all about the transition from the golden heat of the Central Valley to the cooling coastal fog of the Pacific. This dish is a love letter to that bridge—pillowy blue corn masa, charred over almond wood, meeting the sharp, electric chill of a stone fruit aguachile. It’s soft, soulful, and captures the ephemeral magic of a late-summer afternoon in Northern California.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup organic blue corn masa harina
* 3/4 cup warm filtered water
* 2 tablespoons Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
* 1/2 teaspoon flaky sea salt
* 2 large heirloom tomatoes, different colors, diced small
* 2 ripe nectarines or white peaches, sliced into thin wedges
* 1 Fresno chile, seeded and finely minced
* 1/4 cup verjus or white balsamic vinegar
* Juice of 2 Ojai Pixie tangerines (or limes)
* 1 tablespoon fresh cilantro flowers or torn leaves
* 1/2 teaspoon smoked alderwood salt
* 1 small watermelon radish, shaved paper-thin

## Instructions

* In a medium bowl, combine the blue corn masa harina and sea salt. Slowly add the warm water and a splash of olive oil, kneading until a soft, play-dough-like consistency forms. Cover with a damp cloth for 10 minutes.
* Divide the dough into small golf-ball-sized rounds and flatten them into discs about 1/2 inch thick. Pinch the edges upward to create a small rim.
* Heat a cast-iron skillet over a medium-high flame (or an oak-fired grill). Lightly sear the sopes for 3-4 minutes per side until charred spots appear and they feel firm but yielding.
* While the sopes are warm, toss the diced tomatoes, nectarines, and Fresno chiles in a bowl with the verjus and citrus juice. Let the mixture macerate for 5 minutes to create a bright brine.
* Place the warm sopes on a serving plate. Top each with a generous spoonful of the tomato and nectarine mixture, ensuring some of the bright juice soaks into the masa.
* Garnish with shaved radish, cilantro flowers, a final drizzle of Sonoma olive oil, and a pinch of smoked salt.

This dish is meant to be eaten with your hands, leaning over a wooden table while the sun dips below the horizon. It is light enough for a degustation but rich enough in flavor to leave a lasting memory. Enjoy the harvest.
