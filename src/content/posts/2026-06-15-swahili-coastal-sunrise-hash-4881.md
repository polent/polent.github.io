## Introduction

Hujambo! As the Indian Ocean breeze whispers through the palms of Mombasa, I bring you a breakfast that grounds you in the red soil of our highlands while carrying the heat of the trade winds. This savory hash is a marriage of firm matoke green bananas and earthy njahi beans, bathed in the warmth of coastal spices and finished with the bright, acidic snap of fresh kachumbari. It is a plate of home, designed to fuel your spirit for the day ahead.

## Ingredients

* 3 firm green bananas (matoke), peeled and cubed
* 1 cup cooked njahi (black beans)
* 2 tablespoons fresh coconut oil
* 1 red onion, finely diced
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 teaspoon berbere spice blend
* 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
* 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 1 fresh pilipili (African bird's eye chili), finely minced
* 1/2 cup fresh thick coconut milk
* 1 bunch sukuma wiki (collard greens), thinly sliced
* 1/2 cup kachumbari (diced tomatoes, onions, and cilantro)
* 1 fresh lime, halved for squeezing
* Salt to taste

## Instructions

* Heat the coconut oil in a heavy cast-iron pan or tava over medium heat until it shimmers.
* Add the cubed green bananas to the pan and sauté until they develop a golden crust and start to soften, approximately 8 to 10 minutes.
* Stir in the red onion and pilipili chili, cooking until the onions become translucent and fragrant.
* Add the minced garlic, berbere, cumin, and cardamom, stirring for one minute to allow the spices to toast and release their oils.
* Fold in the cooked njahi beans and the sliced sukuma wiki, tossing gently to combine with the spiced bananas.
* Pour the thick coconut milk over the mixture and reduce the heat to low. Simmer for 5 minutes until the liquid thickens into a rich glaze and the greens are tender.
* Remove from the heat and season with salt. Top with a generous heap of fresh kachumbari and a bright squeeze of lime juice just before serving.

Savor each spoonful with a hot cup of spiced chai or a glass of chilled tamarind juice. Let the heat of the pilipili wake your senses and the coconut milk soften your morning. Until we cook together again, karibu chakula—you are most welcome to this table.

*Nia*
