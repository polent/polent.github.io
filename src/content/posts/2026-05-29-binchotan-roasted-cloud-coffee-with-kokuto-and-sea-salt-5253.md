## Introduction

In the quiet of the morning at my Kyoto workshop, I watch the steam rise from the Binchotan coals. Today, I bring the ephemeral nature of the cloud to your cup. While the world seeks the heavy sweetness of modern fluffy coffee, I offer a version rooted in the shojin tradition—refined, balanced, and echoing with the hidden depth of umami. We use Okinawan kokuto for earthiness and a whisper of aged tamari to bridge the bitterness of the roast with the creaminess of our hand-pressed soy milk. It is not just a drink; it is a moment of stillness.

## Ingredients

* 2 tablespoons ultra-fine Binchotan-roasted coffee powder
* 2 tablespoons Okinawan kokuto (crushed black sugar)
* 2 tablespoons hot filtered spring water
* 1 cup freshly pressed, chilled organic soy milk
* 1 drop 10-year aged tamari
* A pinch of Moshio (seaweed-infused sea salt)
* 1 tiny sprig of purple shiso or a single sakura petal for garnish

## Instructions

* In a small hand-carved wooden bowl, combine the coffee powder, crushed kokuto, and hot spring water.
* Using a bamboo whisk or a small aerator, beat the mixture with steady intention until it transforms into a thick, glossy, and stable foam that resembles a dark autumn cloud.
* Prepare your serving vessel—a hand-pinched ceramic cup that feels grounded in your palm.
* Season the chilled soy milk with a single drop of aged tamari to heighten the natural sweetness of the beans.
* Pour the soy milk into the cup over a single clear cube of ice, leaving space at the top.
* Gently spoon the coffee foam over the milk, letting it sit asymmetrically to honor wabi-sabi aesthetics.
* Sprinkle the surface with a tiny pinch of Moshio and garnish with the shiso or sakura petal.
* Serve immediately while the temperature contrast between the cool milk and the room-temperature foam is at its peak.

As the foam slowly merges with the cool soy milk below, remember that beauty lies in the transition. This coffee asks you to slow down, to notice the salt on your tongue and the smoke in the beans. May it bring a sense of wabi-sabi to your day—the perfect appreciation of the imperfect and the fleeting. Bowing in gratitude to the soil and the bean.

*Hiroshi*
