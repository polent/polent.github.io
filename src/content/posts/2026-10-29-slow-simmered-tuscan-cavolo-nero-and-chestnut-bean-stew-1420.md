## Introduction

In my Tuscan kitchen, winter days mean the fire burns low and the terracotta pot bubbles quietly for hours. This soup honors the purest cucina povera tradition: tender cannellini beans simmered slow with sweet roasted chestnuts, deeply earthy garden cavolo nero, and the aromatic perfume of rosemary, finished with a generous swirl of new-press extra virgin olive oil.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup dried cannellini beans, soaked overnight and drained
- 1 large bunch Tuscan cavolo nero (lacinato kale), center ribs stripped and leaves roughly chopped
- 1 cup roasted peeled chestnuts, roughly broken by hand
- 1 medium yellow onion, finely minced
- 2 tender stalks celery, finely chopped
- 2 sweet carrots, finely diced
- 4 cloves garlic, gently crushed
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 2 dried bay leaves
- 6 cups rich vegetable broth or filtered water
- 2 thick slices stale rustic sourdough bread
- 4 tablespoons cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil, plus extra for finishing
- Fine sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste
- 1 teaspoon finely grated organic lemon zest

## Instructions

1. In a heavy terracotta pot or enameled cast-iron Dutch oven, warm two tablespoons of olive oil over medium-low heat. Add the minced onion, celery, carrots, and a pinch of salt. Cook gently for about 15 minutes until the soffritto is sweet, fragrant, and meltingly soft.
2. Add the crushed garlic and rosemary sprigs, stirring for two minutes to let the herb oils release into the fat.
3. Stir in the soaked cannellini beans, bay leaves, and broth. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower the heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly, and allow the pot to simmer undisturbed for about 90 minutes until the beans are completely tender.
4. Remove the rosemary stems and bay leaves. Scoop out one cup of the beans with a splash of cooking liquid, blend until silky and smooth, and stir it back into the pot to create a rich, velvety body.
5. Fold in the chopped cavolo nero and crumbled chestnuts. Continue to simmer uncovered over low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, allowing the dark greens to tenderize and the chestnuts to infuse the broth with mild sweetness.
6. Season generously with sea salt, freshly cracked black pepper, and lemon zest. Place a piece of stale bread at the bottom of each bowl, ladle the thick, steaming soup over the bread, and crown with a lavish pour of peppery green olive oil.

Serve this steaming in rough ceramic bowls over day-old crusty bread, finish with the finest olive oil you possess, and let the quiet, timeless warmth of the Tuscan countryside settle into your soul. Buon appetito!
