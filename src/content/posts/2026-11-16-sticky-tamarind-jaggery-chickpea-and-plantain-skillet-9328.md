## Introduction

Today I am leaning into the cozy, modern comfort trend of sticky skillet dishes, but anchoring it deeply in the ancestral techniques of Gujarat and Kerala. This recipe brings together crispy golden plantains and tender spiced chickpeas coated in a glossy, fiery tamarind-jaggery glaze. We finish it right in a smoking cast-iron skillet with crackled mustard seeds, fragrant curry leaves, and toasted coconut flakes.

## Ingredients

* 2 medium semi-ripe green plantains, peeled and sliced into 1/2-inch half-moons
* 1 1/2 cups cooked chickpeas, drained and thoroughly patted dry
* 2 tablespoons virgin coconut oil, divided
* 1 teaspoon black mustard seeds
* 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
* 10-12 fresh curry leaves
* 1 green bird's eye chili, finely slit
* 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
* 1 teaspoon Kashmiri chili powder
* 1/2 teaspoon ground roasted coriander
* 3 tablespoons jaggery or dark coconut sugar, crushed
* 2 1/2 tablespoons tamarind paste dissolved in 3 tablespoons warm water
* 1 tablespoon ginger, finely grated
* 1/2 teaspoon Himalayan pink salt (adjust to taste)
* 2 tablespoons fresh coconut ribbons or flakes, lightly toasted
* Fresh cilantro leaves and lime wedges for garnish

## Instructions

* Heat one tablespoon of coconut oil in a heavy 10-inch seasoned cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat.
* Add the plantain slices in a single layer and pan-sear for about 3 to 4 minutes per side until deeply golden and tender; transfer to a plate.
* In the same skillet, add the dry chickpeas and toast over medium heat for 4 minutes until the skins blister and turn slightly crisp; remove and set with plantains.
* Lower heat to medium and add the remaining tablespoon of coconut oil. Tip in mustard seeds and cumin seeds, letting them crackle and pop.
* Immediately add curry leaves, green chili, and grated ginger, stirring vigorously for 30 seconds until intensely fragrant.
* Whisk the dissolved tamarind paste, jaggery, turmeric, Kashmiri chili powder, coriander, and salt together in a small bowl, then pour directly into the skillet.
* Let the sauce simmer and reduce rapidly for 1 to 2 minutes until it transforms into a bubbling, glossy, thick syrup.
* Return the plantains and chickpeas to the skillet, tossing gently to coat every piece thoroughly in the sticky lacquer, letting the edges caramelize and stick slightly to the pan.
* Turn off the heat, scatter toasted coconut ribbons and fresh cilantro over the top, and serve immediately straight from the warm skillet.

Serve this vibrant skillet bubbling hot with warm rotis, steamed red matta rice, or simply eat it straight from the pan with a squeeze of fresh lime. The way the edges caramelize into chewy, aromatic bites is pure kitchen alchemy.
