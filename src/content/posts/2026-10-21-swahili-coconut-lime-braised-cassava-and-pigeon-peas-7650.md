## Introduction

Welcoming the coastal breeze from Mombasa, this comforting bowl unites tender cassava roots and buttery pigeon peas simmered slowly in rich coconut milk, cut through with the vibrant zing of charred coastal lime and fragrant cracked cardamom.

## Ingredients

* 500g fresh cassava root, peeled, woody core removed, and cut into bite-sized chunks
* 1 cup cooked pigeon peas (mbaazi)
* 1 can (400ml) full-fat coconut milk
* 1 cup vegetable broth
* 2 fresh coastal limes (zest of both, juice of one, one halved for charring)
* 1 medium red onion, finely diced
* 3 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated
* 1 fresh red pilipili chile, thinly sliced
* 1 teaspoon ground cumin
* 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
* 1/2 teaspoon freshly crushed green cardamom seeds
* 1/4 teaspoon whole fenugreek seeds
* 1 tablespoon virgin coconut oil
* 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, stems and leaves separated and chopped
* 1 teaspoon fine sea salt, to taste
* 2 tablespoons roasted crushed groundnuts (peanuts), for garnish

## Instructions

1. Warm the coconut oil in a heavy-bottomed clay pot over medium heat, then sear the halved lime cut-side down until deeply browned and caramelized, about 3 minutes, then set aside.
2. In the residual aromatic oil, toss in the fenugreek seeds until they crackle, then add the diced red onion, ginger, garlic, cilantro stems, and sliced pilipili chile.
3. Sauté for 4 to 5 minutes until soft and fragrant, then incorporate the ground cumin, turmeric, and crushed cardamom, blooming the spices for 1 minute.
4. Add the cassava pieces and pigeon peas, stirring gently to coat them with the spiced paste.
5. Pour in the coconut milk and vegetable broth, bringing the pot to a steady simmer.
6. Cover and braise over low heat for 20 to 25 minutes, until the cassava becomes melt-in-the-mouth tender and naturally thickens the sauce.
7. Uncover, stir in the lime zest, lime juice, and sea salt, letting the stew simmer for 3 minutes to fuse the bright citrus with the rich coconut base.
8. Ladle into warm bowls and finish with chopped cilantro leaves, crushed groundnuts, and the caramelized lime half.

Ladle this fragrant, citrus-laced stew into warm clay bowls alongside griddled tava chapatis or steamed grains, and let the gentle heat of pilipili and the bright lime zest comfort you from the inside out.
