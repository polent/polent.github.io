## Introduction

Benvenuti a tavola! Today in my kitchen, the air smells of woodsmoke and the rich, earthy scent of mushrooms braising. In Tuscany, we say that a good panino is not just a snack; it is a celebration of what the earth yields. I have taken the humble portobello and the resilient cavolo nero from my garden to create a sandwich that feels like a warm hug from the hills. This is my braised gourmet panino, where the forest meets the orto.

## Ingredients

* 4 large portobello mushrooms, thickly sliced
* 1 cup walnut halves, roughly crushed
* 1 bunch cavolo nero (Tuscan kale), ribs removed and torn
* 2 cups cooked cannellini beans
* 1 head of roasted garlic, cloves squeezed out
* 1 cup dry red wine (preferably a Chianti)
* 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
* 1 teaspoon dried chili flakes
* 4 thick slices of sourdough or artisan focaccia
* 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil (new-press if possible)
* Salt and cracked black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* In a heavy-bottomed pan, heat two tablespoons of olive oil and sear the portobello slices and crushed walnuts until golden brown.
* Add the rosemary sprigs and pour in the red wine, scraping the bottom of the pan to release the flavor. Lower the heat and simmer gently until the wine has reduced to a thick, glossy glaze coating the mushrooms.
* While the mushrooms braise, blanch the cavolo nero in boiling salted water for 3 minutes, then drain and sauté in a pan with olive oil, garlic, and chili flakes until tender and vibrant.
* To make the crema, blend the cannellini beans with the roasted garlic and a splash of warm water until silky smooth. Season generously with salt and pepper.
* Toast the bread slices over a flame or in a hot pan until charred at the edges.
* Assemble the panino by spreading a thick layer of bean crema on the bottom slice, followed by the garlicky cavolo nero, and a generous heap of the wine-braised mushrooms and walnuts.
* Top with the second bread slice, press down gently to let the juices mingle, and finish with a final thread of raw extra virgin olive oil.

Serve this while the bread is still warm and the juices are soaking into the crusty crumb. It is simple, honest, and unapologetically Italian. A drizzle of your best oil is the final touch it deserves. Buon appetito!

*Isabella*
