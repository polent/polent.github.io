## Introduction

Karibu! Welcome to my coastal kitchen. Today, we are taking the global gourmet burger trend and grounding it in the red soil of our highlands and the salt of the Indian Ocean. This isn't just a burger; it's a celebration of njahi black beans and the deep, dark sweetness of tamarind-glazed onions, all nestled in a cardamom-scented bun that echoes the mahamri of our morning markets.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup cooked njahi (black beans), slightly mashed
* 1/2 cup roasted groundnuts (peanuts), coarsely crushed
* 1/2 cup cassava flour or sourdough breadcrumbs
* 1 tablespoon berbere spice blend
* 2 large red onions, thinly sliced into rings
* 2 tablespoons tamarind pulp concentrate
* 1 tablespoon coconut sugar or jaggery
* 4 vegan cardamom-infused buns or sourdough rolls
* 2 cups sukuma wiki (collard greens), finely shredded
* 1 cup fresh kachumbari (diced tomatoes, red onion, coriander, and lime juice)
* 1/4 cup thick coconut cream mixed with a squeeze of lime and a pinch of salt
* 2 tablespoons cold-pressed coconut oil for frying
* Sea salt and cracked black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* Begin by caramelizing the onions: In a heavy pan over medium-low heat, sauté the red onions in a little coconut oil until they become soft and translucent. Add the tamarind pulp and coconut sugar, stirring occasionally for 20 minutes until they reach a dark, jam-like consistency. Set aside.
* Prepare the patties: In a large bowl, combine the mashed njahi beans, crushed groundnuts, berbere spice, and cassava flour. Season with salt and pepper. Shape into four thick patties and let them rest in the fridge for 15 minutes to firm up.
* Cook the greens: Lightly sauté the sukuma wiki in a hot pan with a splash of water and salt until just wilted but still vibrant green.
* Sear the burgers: Heat coconut oil on a cast-iron tava or skillet. Fry the njahi patties for about 4-5 minutes on each side until a deep, charred crust forms.
* Toast the buns: Slice the buns and place them cut-side down on the tava for a minute until golden and fragrant with cardamom.
* Assemble the burger: Spread a generous layer of the lime-coconut cream on the bottom bun. Layer with a heap of sukuma wiki, then the njahi patty. Top with a heavy portion of the tamarind-caramelized onions and a spoonful of fresh kachumbari.
* Close the burger and press down slightly to let the juices meld before serving immediately.

As the sun sets over the Mombasa harbor, let the warmth of the berbere spice and the tang of the kachumbari remind you that even a simple burger can be a journey. Serve this with a glass of chilled hibiscus juice and enjoy the spirit of the Swahili coast. Mapishi mema!

*Nia*
