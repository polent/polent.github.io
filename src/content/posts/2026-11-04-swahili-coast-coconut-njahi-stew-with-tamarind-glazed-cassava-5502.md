## Introduction

Habari! When I think of a coastal buffet spread, I think of the warmth rising from heavy clay pots lining our Mombasa tables. This centerpiece dish distills that lavish abundance into one plate, bringing together the deep, earthy richness of heirloom njahi beans, buttery braised cassava root, and fresh morning-pressed coconut milk, all balanced with sour tamarind, fiery pilipili, and aromatic crushed cardamom.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups cooked njahi (black dolichos or black turtle beans)
- 1 large cassava root, peeled, fibrous core removed, cut into bite-sized chunks
- 2 cups freshly pressed thick coconut milk
- 1 cup light coconut milk or vegetable broth
- 3 tablespoons tamarind paste, dissolved in 1/4 cup warm water
- 2 fresh red pilipili chiles, finely minced
- 1 large red onion, thinly sliced
- 4 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated
- 1 teaspoon whole cumin seeds, lightly bruised
- 1 teaspoon green cardamom pods, seeds removed and crushed
- 1/2 teaspoon ground fenugreek
- 1/2 teaspoon berbere spice blend
- 2 tablespoons cold-pressed coconut oil
- 1 bunch sukuma wiki (collard greens), finely ribboned
- 1 cup fresh kachumbari (diced plum tomatoes, red onion, fresh coriander, lime juice, and sea salt)
- Fine sea salt to taste

## Instructions

1. Steam or parboil the cassava chunks in salted water for roughly 12 minutes until fork-tender but structurally intact, then drain.
2. Heat one tablespoon of coconut oil in a wide skillet over medium-high heat, add the cassava chunks, and sear until their edges turn golden.
3. Pour the dissolved tamarind paste and half of the minced pilipili chiles over the cassava, tossing continuously until sticky, glossy, and caramelized, then set aside.
4. Warm the remaining coconut oil in a heavy clay pot or deep pan, then sauté the sliced red onions until soft and translucent with caramelized borders.
5. Stir in the garlic, ginger, cumin, cardamom, fenugreek, and berbere blend, toasting for 60 seconds until the aroma fills the room.
6. Add the cooked njahi beans along with the light coconut milk, season with sea salt, and simmer uncovered for 10 minutes to infuse the legumes.
7. Pour in the thick coconut milk and gently nestle the tamarind-glazed cassava into the gravy, lowering the heat to a gentle simmer for 8 minutes until thickened and silky.
8. Quickly flash-fry or wilt the ribboned sukuma wiki in a dry skillet for 90 seconds to preserve its deep green color and light crunch.
9. Ladle the bean stew into wide ceramic serving bowls, crown with the tamarind-glazed cassava, wilted greens, and generous spoonfuls of fresh kachumbari relish before serving.

Serve this steaming coastal centerpiece alongside warm, tava-charred chapati or fluffy mahamri to scoop up every drop of spiced coconut cream. It carries the soul of our shoreline and highland soil straight to your gathering. Karibu chakula!
