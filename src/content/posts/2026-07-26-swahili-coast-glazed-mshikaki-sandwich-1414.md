## Introduction

Karibu! Today we are marrying the rustic soul of the Kenyan highlands with the refined sweetness of the Swahili coast. This glazed gourmet sandwich takes the concept of street-side mshikaki and elevates it with a sticky tamarind-berbere reduction and the crunch of roasted groundnuts. It is a handheld feast that tastes of the Indian Ocean breeze and the red soil of home.

## Ingredients

- 2 thick slices of artisanal sourdough or teff-blended bread
- 250g King Oyster mushrooms, sliced into thick 'steaks'
- 3 tablespoons tamarind concentrate
- 2 tablespoons date syrup or maple syrup
- 1 teaspoon berbere spice blend
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
- 1/4 teaspoon pilipili (African bird's eye chili) flakes
- 1 cup sukuma wiki (collard greens or kale), finely shredded
- 2 tablespoons roasted groundnuts (peanuts), roughly crushed
- 1/2 red onion, sliced into thin rings
- 1 firm tomato, finely diced
- Fresh coriander leaves and a squeeze of lime
- 1 tablespoon coconut oil for searing
- A smear of roasted garlic and coconut cream spread

## Instructions

- In a small bowl, whisk together the tamarind concentrate, date syrup, berbere, cardamom, and pilipili to create the signature glaze.
- Heat a cast-iron tava or skillet with coconut oil over medium-high heat.
- Arrange the mushroom steaks in the pan and sear until they are golden and the edges begin to char.
- Brush the mushrooms generously with the tamarind glaze, turning them frequently for 2 minutes until they are sticky, dark, and caramelized.
- Lightly toast your bread slices on the same pan to pick up the residual spices and oils.
- Quickly toss the shredded sukuma wiki in the pan for 30 seconds just to wilt it while keeping its vibrant green color.
- Spread the coconut garlic cream on the bottom slice of bread, then layer the wilted greens and the crushed groundnuts.
- Stack the glazed mshikaki mushrooms on top, followed by a fresh kachumbari made of red onion, tomato, coriander, and lime juice.
- Close the sandwich, press down gently so the juices soak into the crumb, and slice diagonally.

As the tamarind glaze settles into the warm bread, take a moment to breathe in the cardamom and clove. This sandwich is more than a meal; it is a map of our trade routes and a tribute to the vibrant, plant-based bounty of East Africa. Enjoy every spicy, tangy bite!
