## Introduction

Karibu! In my Mombasa kitchen, the air always smells of salt and roasting spices. Today, I am bringing the red soil of the highlands and the blue of the Indian Ocean together in a single bite. These croquettes transform the humble, earthy njahi bean and creamy cassava into a sophisticated finger food, spiked with the fiery warmth of berbere and cooled by the morning's fresh coconut milk. They are small, punchy, and perfect for sharing as the sun begins to dip below the horizon.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups boiled and mashed cassava root
* 1 cup cooked njahi (black beans), lightly crushed
* 2 green pilipili chiles, finely minced
* 1 teaspoon toasted cumin seeds
* 1 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 1/2 cup sukuma wiki (collard greens), very finely shredded
* 1/2 cup roasted groundnuts, crushed into a coarse meal
* 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
* 1 teaspoon berbere spice blend
* 1 tablespoon coconut sugar
* 1/2 cup thick fresh coconut milk
* Juice of 1 lime
* A pinch of sea salt

## Instructions

* In a large clay bowl, combine the mashed cassava, crushed njahi beans, pilipili chiles, cumin, cardamom, and shredded sukuma wiki until well incorporated.
* Season the mixture with a pinch of sea salt and mix thoroughly.
* Roll the mixture into small, bite-sized cylinders or rounds, about the size of a walnut.
* Dredge each croquette in the crushed roasted groundnuts, pressing firmly so the nuts adhere to create a textured crust.
* Heat a shallow layer of oil in a tava or heavy pan over medium heat. Fry the croquettes until they are golden brown and crispy on all sides.
* In a small bowl, whisk together the tamarind paste, berbere spice, and coconut sugar to create a thick glaze.
* Lightly brush the warm croquettes with the berbere-tamarind glaze while they are still hot.
* For the dipping sauce, whisk the fresh coconut milk with lime juice and a tiny pinch of salt.
* Arrange the croquettes on a platter and serve immediately with the coconut-lime dip on the side.

These bites are more than just food; they are the story of our trade winds and our resilient earth. I hope they bring a bit of the Swahili coast’s warmth to your table today. Serve them hot, share them widely, and let the spices linger. Until next time, stay seasoned.

*Nia*
