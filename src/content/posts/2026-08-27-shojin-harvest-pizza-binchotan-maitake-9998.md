## Introduction

In the quiet corners of Kyoto, we learn that even a dish as global as pizza can become a vessel for shojin ryori. Today, I offer you a meditation on fire and fermentation. This artisan pizza honors the essence of shun by using earth-grown ingredients treated with the reverence of a temple meal. We use a slow-fermented sourdough to mirror the patience of the seasons, topped with treasures from the forest floor charred over binchotan coals. It is a warm, rustic embrace that celebrates the harmony between tradition and the present moment.

## Ingredients

* 250g Sourdough pizza dough, 72-hour cold fermented
* 100g Maitake mushrooms, hand-torn to preserve texture
* 50g Takenoko (young bamboo shoots), sliced paper-thin using katsuramuki technique
* 3 tbsp Shiro miso (white miso), house-fermented
* 100ml Silken tofu, pressed and blended into a smooth cream
* 1 sheet Fresh Yuba (bean curd skin), torn into delicate ribbons
* 1 tsp Sansho pepper berries, lightly bruised
* 1 tbsp Kombu-infused extra virgin olive oil
* A handful of fresh Mizuna or Shiso leaves
* A pinch of flaked sea salt

## Instructions

* Begin by preheating a pizza stone in your oven at its highest setting for at least one hour, or prepare your binchotan charcoal grill for high-heat indirect cooking.
* Lightly sear the maitake mushrooms and bamboo shoots over the binchotan coals until the edges are curled and kissed with smoke.
* In a small cedar bowl, whisk the shiro miso into the silken tofu cream until it reaches the consistency of a thick, pale velvet.
* On a floured surface, gently stretch the sourdough dough by hand, being careful not to bruise the air pockets earned through long fermentation.
* Spread the miso-tofu cream over the base in a thin, intentional layer, leaving a wide margin for the crust to rise.
* Arrange the smoked mushrooms, bamboo shoots, and yuba ribbons across the cream in an asymmetrical, natural pattern.
* Slide the pizza onto the hot stone and bake until the crust is blistered with leopard spots and the yuba has turned slightly crisp.
* Remove from the heat and immediately drizzle with the kombu-infused oil and a dusting of sansho pepper.
* Garnish with fresh greens just before serving to maintain their vibrant life force.

As you finish this meal, take a moment to appreciate the charcoal’s breath and the grain’s transformation. This pizza is not merely food, but a dialogue between the element of fire and the gift of the earth. I hope it brings a sense of wabi-sabi to your table—perfect in its imperfections. Until the seasons shift again, keep your heart open and your dashi clear.

*Hiroshi*
