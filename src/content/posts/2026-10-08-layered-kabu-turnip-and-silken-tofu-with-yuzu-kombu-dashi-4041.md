## Introduction

Today tasting course centers on the humble kabu turnip—sweet, translucent, and receptive to the quiet power of aged cedar-cured tamari. In this layered composition, paper-thin shaved sheets of binchotan-kissed kabu alternate with freshly curdled silken tofu, suspended in a warm, pristine kombu dashi scented with winter yuzu peel.

## Ingredients

* 2 medium Japanese kabu turnips, scrubbed and peeled
* 200g freshly made silken tofu (kinugoshi), gently drained and pressed
* 1 piece Rishiri kombu (about 10g), wiped gently with a damp cloth
* 3 cups cold soft spring water
* 1 tablespoon aged artisanal tamari
* 1 tablespoon dry vegan junmai sake
* 1 teaspoon naturally fermented white shiro miso
* 1 small yuzu, peel shaved into delicate pine-needle slivers
* 1 teaspoon cold-pressed roasted sesame oil
* 4 purple shiso blossoms, for finishing
* 1 pinch fine sea salt (moshio)

## Instructions

* Prepare the ichiban kombu dashi by steeping the Rishiri kombu in cold spring water for at least 4 hours, then gently heating over very low flame until small bubbles form along the edges; remove the kelp and keep the broth at a gentle 80°C.
* Perform paper-thin katsuramuki style slicing on the kabu turnips, carving them into uniform 2 mm delicate circular rounds.
* Lightly brush half of the turnip discs with roasted sesame oil and sear briefly over glowing binchotan charcoal or an intensely hot dry cast-iron skillet for 20 seconds per side until faint blistered specks appear.
* Simmer the remaining uncharred turnip discs gently in one cup of the kombu dashi seasoned with sake and moshio salt until tender and translucent, about 3 minutes.
* Season the remaining warm kombu dashi with aged tamari and dissolved white miso, straining through layered cheesecloth for absolute clarity.
* Carefully portion the pressed silken tofu into uniform matching circles.
* In the center of warm wabi-sabi ceramic bowls, assemble the mille-feuille by alternating charred turnip, simmered turnip, and delicate silken tofu in neat layers.
* Ladle the hot yuzu-infused dashi gently around the base of the tower, crowning each presentation with shaved yuzu slivers and fresh shiso blossoms.

Serve immediately in a hand-thrown chawan ceramic bowl. Let each layer melt together, carrying the deep soil of Kyoto and the crisp breath of early seasonal frost into a solitary, warming moment.

*Hiroshi*
