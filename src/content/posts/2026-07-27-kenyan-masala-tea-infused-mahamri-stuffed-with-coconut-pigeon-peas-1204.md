## Introduction

Karibu! In the heart of Mombasa, the morning air is thick with the scent of woodsmoke and brewing ginger tea. Today, I am bringing you a soulful evolution of our coastal breakfast. We are taking the classic mahamri—our cardamom-scented coconut doughnut—and infusing the dough itself with a concentrated reduction of Kenyan black tea and masala spices. We then stuff these golden pockets with creamy mbaazi (pigeon peas) simmered in fresh coconut milk. This is the essence of a Swahili tea break, reimagined as a handheld treasure that tastes of the Indian Ocean breeze.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups all-purpose flour
* 1/4 cup sugar
* 1 teaspoon dry yeast
* 1 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 1/2 cup thick coconut milk
* 1/4 cup strong brewed Kenyan black tea, cooled
* 1 cup cooked pigeon peas (mbaazi)
* 1 cup heavy coconut cream
* 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
* 1 small green pilipili chile, minced
* Salt to taste
* Vegetable oil for deep frying
* Fresh lime and kachumbari (tomato and onion salad) for serving

## Instructions

* In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, yeast, and cardamom until well combined.
* Gradually add the coconut milk and the cooled black tea reduction to the flour mixture. Knead until you have a smooth, elastic dough. Cover and let it rest in a warm place for about an hour or until doubled in size.
* While the dough rises, prepare the filling. In a clay pot or small saucepan, combine the cooked pigeon peas, heavy coconut cream, turmeric, and minced pilipili.
* Simmer the peas over low heat, stirring occasionally, until the coconut cream reduces to a very thick, jam-like consistency that coats the peas. Season with salt and set aside to cool.
* Divide the risen dough into small balls. Roll each ball into a circle, place a tablespoon of the cooled pigeon pea mixture in the center, and fold the dough over to create a semi-circle or triangle, pinching the edges firmly to seal.
* Heat the oil in a deep pan until a small piece of dough sizzles immediately.
* Fry the stuffed mahamri in batches until they are golden brown and puffed on both sides. Drain on paper towels.
* Serve warm with a side of zesty kachumbari and an extra squeeze of lime to cut through the richness.

As you take a bite, let the warmth of the cardamom and the earthy depth of the tea-infused dough ground you. These stuffed treats are best enjoyed while the coconut cream is still velvety and the kachumbari is fresh. From my coastal kitchen to yours, may your day be as rich and vibrant as these flavors. Enjoy!

*Nia*
