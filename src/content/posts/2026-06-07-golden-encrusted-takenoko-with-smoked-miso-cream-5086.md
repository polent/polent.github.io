## Introduction

In the quiet of the Kyoto morning, I often contemplate the transition of seasons through the texture of a single ingredient. Today, inspired by the vibrant energy of the festival spirit, I bring you a dish that balances the grounded nature of the earth with a celebratory crispness. This encrusted bamboo shoot is a meditation on the fleeting moment when the shoot is tender enough to yield but firm enough to carry the weight of a toasted sesame shell. It is a dialogue between the softness of the forest floor and the crackle of a festival fire.

## Ingredients

* 2 medium fresh bamboo shoots (takenoko), pre-boiled in rice bran water
* 500ml cold-brew kombu dashi
* 2 tablespoons aged tamari
* 1 tablespoon mirin
* 50g roasted white sesame seeds, lightly crushed
* 50g vegan rice cracker crumbs (senbei), finely pulsed
* 1 tablespoon brown rice flour
* 2 tablespoons ice-cold water
* High-smoke point neutral oil for flash-frying
* 30g white miso, fermented for at least 6 months
* 1 tablespoon fresh silken tofu, blended until smooth
* 1 drop binchotan-infused oil or liquid smoke
* 2 fresh sansho leaves or sprigs of kinome

## Instructions

* Begin by simmering the bamboo shoots in the kombu dashi, tamari, and mirin for 20 minutes over a low flame to allow the umami to penetrate the core of the vegetable.
* Remove the shoots and pat them dry with a cotton cloth, handling them with the reverence one would show a sacred scroll.
* Whisk the brown rice flour and ice-cold water in a small bowl to create a light, translucent slurry.
* In a separate shallow vessel, combine the crushed sesame seeds and the rice cracker crumbs, mixing them until they look like the speckled stones of a riverbed.
* Carefully dip each bamboo shoot into the slurry, then dredge it through the seed and cracker mixture, pressing gently to ensure an even, protective crust.
* Heat the oil in a small cast-iron pot until it reaches 180C. Flash-fry the encrusted shoots for 60 seconds, only until the exterior is golden and resonant.
* For the cream, fold the white miso and the binchotan-infused oil into the smooth silken tofu until the texture is like heavy silk.
* Place the bamboo shoots on an asymmetric ceramic plate, leaving space for the eye to rest. Add a small pool of the smoked miso cream and garnish with a single sansho leaf to invite the scent of the mountains.

As you taste this dish, let the crunch remind you of the ephemeral nature of joy and the soft center remind you of the peace found in the temple gardens. Food is not merely sustenance; it is a way to harmonize our inner spirit with the outer world. I hope this small offering brings a moment of stillness to your busy day. May your heart be as light as the falling sakura.

*Hiroshi*
