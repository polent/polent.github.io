## Introduction

Hujambo! Today I am bringing the warmth of the Mombasa sun to your lunch hour with a dish that bridges the gap between traditional Swahili slow-cooking and the modern pace of life. This bento box is a journey from the red soils of the highlands to the salty Indian Ocean breeze, featuring bold njahi beans and tangy tamarind spikes that dance on the tongue. It is a vibrant, portable feast designed to nourish the soul and spark the senses.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup dried njahi black beans soaked overnight
* 2 cups fresh coconut milk pressed from the husk
* 1 tablespoon berbere spice blend for that Ethiopian heat
* 2 green matoke bananas peeled and cubed
* 1 cup long-grain basmati rice
* 4 green cardamom pods lightly crushed
* 1 small red onion finely diced
* 2 firm plum tomatoes chopped
* 1 tablespoon thick tamarind concentrate
* 2 fresh pilipili chiles minced
* 1 bunch fresh cilantro leaves chopped
* 2 juicy limes halved
* 1/4 cup roasted groundnuts roughly crushed
* 1 teaspoon sea salt

## Instructions

* Drain the soaked njahi beans and place them in a clay pot or heavy saucepan with enough water to cover. Simmer until tender but not mushy.
* Drain the excess water from the beans and stir in 1 cup of coconut milk, the berbere spice, and half of the diced onions. Simmer on low heat until the sauce is thick and coats the beans beautifully.
* In a separate pot, combine the rice, crushed cardamom pods, remaining coconut milk, and a pinch of salt. Cook until the rice is fluffy and smells of the spice trade winds.
* Steam the green banana cubes until they are fork-tender. While still hot, toss them in a bowl with the tamarind concentrate, one minced pilipili, and a squeeze of lime juice.
* Create a fresh kachumbari by mixing the remaining onions, tomatoes, cilantro, and the second pilipili. Season with lime juice and salt.
* Arrange the bento box by placing the cardamom coconut rice in the main section. Top with the rich njahi stew and a generous sprinkle of crushed groundnuts.
* Fill the side compartments with the tamarind-spiked green bananas and the vibrant kachumbari relish.
* Serve at room temperature to allow the spices to fully bloom.

As the sun sets over the dhows in the harbor, enjoy this box with a cold glass of hibiscus tea or a simple lime water. It is a taste of my home, reimagined for your modern table. Karibuni chakula—welcome to the table!
