## Introduction

Welcome back to the Black Forest workshop. Today, we are taking hand-scraped Spätzle straight to the hot cast-iron skillet, searing them until golden and crisp-edged alongside earthy beechwood-smoked king oyster mushrooms, caramelized leeks, and tangy fermented red cabbage.

## Ingredients

* 300 g Spätzle flour (or half coarse durum semolina, half all-purpose flour)
* 180 ml sparkling mineral water
* 1 tbsp chickpea flour mixed with 2 tbsp water
* 1/2 tsp ground turmeric (for golden color)
* 1 tsp fine sea salt
* 2 tbsp cold-pressed rapeseed oil
* 200 g king oyster mushrooms, torn into thick shreds
* 1 leek, white and light green parts thinly sliced
* 100 g lacto-fermented red cabbage, well drained
* 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
* 1 tbsp coarse German whole-grain mustard
* 1 tbsp nutritional yeast
* 1 pinch smoked sea salt
* 2 sprigs fresh mountain marjoram, leaves stripped

## Instructions

* Whisk Spätzle flour, chickpea slurry, turmeric, sea salt, and sparkling water in a bowl vigorously until a glossy, elastic batter forms with air bubbles; let rest for 10 minutes.
* Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil, scrape the batter from a board or press through a Spätzle press into the water, and simmer for 2 minutes until floating; drain immediately and toss with a drop of rapeseed oil.
* Heat a heavy cast-iron skillet over high heat with half the rapeseed oil, add the shredded king oyster mushrooms, and sear untouched for 3 minutes until deeply browned and woodsy; season with smoked sea salt and remove.
* Add the remaining oil to the pan, add the cooked Spätzle in an even layer, and press down lightly to achieve crispy, golden, skillet-seared edges.
* Fold in the sliced leeks and garlic, sautéing for 2 minutes until tender-crisp.
* Return mushrooms to the pan, stir in the coarse mustard and nutritional yeast, then toss through the lacto-fermented red cabbage right before taking off the heat to warm through without losing its probiotic crispness.
* Scatter with fresh wild marjoram and cracked black pepper, serving directly from the rustic skillet.

Serve this smoking hot straight from the pan, scattering fresh marjoram over top. It is simple, woodsy comfort food elevated by fermentation and high-heat craft.
