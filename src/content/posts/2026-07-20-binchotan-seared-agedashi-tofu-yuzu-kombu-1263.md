## Introduction

Welcome. As the morning mist clings to the moss of the temple garden, I find myself drawn to the quiet transition of the seasons. Today’s special is a meditation on texture and temperature. We take the creaminess of fresh silken tofu, protect it with a delicate veil of potato starch, and offer it to the heat. This dish, a Binchotan-Seared Agedashi Tofu, is balanced by the brightness of yuzu and the deep, ocean-breath of cold-steeped kombu dashi. It is simple, yet it contains the world.

## Ingredients

- 300g Fresh silken tofu, pressed gently for 20 minutes
- 50g Katakuriko (potato starch) for dredging
- 200ml Kombu dashi, cold-steeped for 12 hours
- 2 tbsp Aged tamari
- 1 tbsp Organic mirin
- 50g Daikon radish, finely grated (momiji oroshi style)
- 1 tsp Fresh yuzu zest, shaved thinly
- 1 sprig Fresh shiso or seasonal micro-greens
- Cold-pressed rapeseed oil for shallow frying
- A small piece of Binchotan charcoal (optional, for finishing aroma)

## Instructions

- Prepare the dashi by combining the kombu stock, tamari, and mirin in a small copper pot. Bring to a bare simmer to marry the flavors, then keep warm on the lowest flame.
- Cut the silken tofu into four elegant cubes. Pat the surfaces with extreme care using a linen cloth; the tofu must be dry but remain unbruised.
- Lightly dredge each cube in the katakuriko, shaking off every excess grain. The coating should be a mere ghost of a veil, invisible until it touches the oil.
- Heat the rapeseed oil to 170°C in a cast iron pan. Shallow fry the tofu cubes, turning them gently until the starch becomes translucent and crisp. Do not allow them to brown heavily; we seek a crystalline pale crunch.
- If using binchotan, briefly hold the glowing charcoal above the tofu to impart a whisper of smoke without scorching the starch.
- Place one or two cubes into an asymmetric, dark-glazed ceramic bowl.
- Gently pour the warm dashi around the base of the tofu, being careful to keep the top of the tofu dry and crisp.
- Crown the tofu with a mound of the grated daikon and the yuzu zest. Finish with a single shiso leaf placed with intention.

Eat while the steam still dances. Let the contrast between the crisp exterior and the melting heart remind you of the fleeting nature of all things. I hope this small bowl brings you a moment of stillness in your busy day.

*Hiroshi*
