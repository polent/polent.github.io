## Introduction

In the quiet of the morning, I find that the most profound flavors are often the simplest. Today, we bridge the gap between the refined silence of the temple and the vibrant energy of the street. This dish celebrates the grassy, earth-bound essence of spring bamboo—takenoko—grilled over white charcoal to evoke the warmth of a Kyoto evening market.

## Ingredients

- 2 medium fresh bamboo shoots (takenoko), parboiled with rice bran (nuka) to remove astringency
- 12 fresh green shiso leaves, finely minced
- 3 tablespoons white miso (shiro miso), ideally house-fermented
- 1 tablespoon aged tamari
- 1 tablespoon organic mirin
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- 1/2 teaspoon sansho pepper (Japanese prickly ash)
- 1 tablespoon toasted white sesame seeds
- Bamboo skewers, soaked in water for 30 minutes

## Instructions

- Cut the pre-boiled bamboo shoots into thick, vertical wedges, ensuring the delicate inner layers remain intact.
- In a small stone bowl, whisk together the white miso, aged tamari, and mirin until the texture is silken and the color is a pale amber.
- Use your knife to perform a fine mince on the shiso leaves, then stir them into the sesame oil to create a bright, grassy paste.
- Thread the bamboo wedges onto the soaked skewers, three to a stick, treating each piece with reverence for its shape.
- Ignite the binchotan charcoal. Once it reaches a steady, glowing heat, place the skewers over the grill.
- Sear the bamboo for 3-4 minutes on each side until charred spots appear, releasing an earthy, toasted aroma.
- During the final minute of grilling, brush the miso glaze onto the bamboo. Let it bubble and caramelize slightly over the coals.
- Remove from heat and immediately top with a dollop of the fresh shiso pesto.
- Garnish with a light dusting of sansho pepper and sesame seeds before serving on a cold, unglazed ceramic plate.

This skewer is a fleeting meditation on the transition of seasons. The bitterness of the greens and the sweetness of the miso are the breath of the earth itself. I hope this brings a moment of zen to your day.

*Hiroshi*
