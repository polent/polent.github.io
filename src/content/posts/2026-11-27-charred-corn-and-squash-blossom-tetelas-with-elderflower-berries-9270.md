## Introduction

Nothing sings Northern California springtime quite like pairing sweet, early-season stone fruit and crisp California avocados with peppery garden blossoms. This charred sweet corn and roasted squash blossom tetela is wrapped in heritage heirloom masa, griddled over open flame, and adorned with elderflower-marinated blackberries, nasturtiums, and silky avocado crema. It is coastal, floral, and deeply grounded in earthy masa magic.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups heirloom blue or yellow nixtamalized masa harina
* 1 1/2 cups warm spring water
* 1/2 tsp sea salt
* 8 fresh squash blossoms, stems trimmed and stamens removed
* 1 ear sweet corn, husked and charred over open flame
* 1 cup cooked Rancho Gordo black beans, lightly smashed
* 1 cup fresh blackberries, halved
* 1 tbsp elderflower cordial or botanical vinegar
* 1 tbsp cold-pressed Sonoma extra virgin olive oil
* 1 ripe Haas avocado, blended with lime juice and sea salt until smooth
* A handful of edible nasturtium petals, borage, and calendula
* Flaky sea salt and crushed pink peppercorns for finishing

## Instructions

* In a small ceramic bowl, toss halved blackberries with elderflower cordial, olive oil, and a pinch of flaky salt; set aside to macerate and develop floral aromas.
* Slice charred corn kernels off the cob and fold together with the lightly smashed black beans, seasoned with a touch of salt and lime.
* Mix masa harina, warm water, and sea salt in a mixing bowl until it reaches the texture of soft, pliable clay. Divide into 8 equal balls.
* Press each masa ball between parchment sheets using a tortilla press into a thin round. Lay one cleaned squash blossom flat in the center, top with 2 tablespoons of the charred corn and bean mixture.
* Gently fold three edges inward over the filling to form a triangle pocket (tetela), pressing edges lightly to seal.
* Heat a dry cast-iron comal or skillet over medium-high heat. Cook tetelas for about 3 to 4 minutes per side, until lightly charred, puffed, and fully cooked through.
* Spoon silky avocado crema onto a warm ceramic platter, set the hot tetelas over the sauce, and spoon the macerated elderflower blackberries around them.
* Scatter with fresh nasturtiums, borage flowers, and a sprinkle of crushed pink peppercorns before serving immediately.

Serve these fresh off the comal while the pockets of squash blossoms are tender and fragrant. They make the ultimate sunlit lunch with a crisp glass of Mendocino verjus spritz or dry rosé. Enjoy this vibrant slice of market goodness!
