## Introduction

In my kitchen in the hills of Tuscany, nothing is ever wasted, and patience is our most sacred ingredient. This fermented bento box bridges our ancient cucina povera traditions with mindful everyday eating. Drawing together naturally fermented farro cakes, house-made cannellini bean tempeh glazed in sweet aged balsamic, tangy lacto-fermented giardiniera, and tender cavolo nero touched by our freshest cold-pressed olive oil, it offers a lunch that is deeply restorative, rustic, and alive.

## Ingredients

* 1 cup pearled farro, soaked overnight in water with 1 tbsp sourdough starter or raw apple cider vinegar, drained and rinsed
* 1 cup light vegetable broth
* 1 cup water
* 1 fresh rosemary sprig
* 1 bay leaf
* 200 g cannellini bean tempeh, sliced into bite-sized cubes
* 2 tbsp aged balsamic vinegar of Modena
* 1 tbsp fermented gluten-free tamari
* 1 cup homemade lacto-fermented giardiniera (fennel wedges, radishes, and Romanesco florets)
* 1 bunch cavolo nero (Tuscan kale), stems stripped and leaves chopped
* 2 cloves lacto-fermented garlic, minced into a smooth paste
* 2 tbsp toasted Italian pine nuts
* 3 tbsp cultured almond ricotta
* 1 tsp Sicilian capers, rinsed
* 3 tbsp Tuscan extra virgin olive oil
* Flaky sea salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

## Instructions

* Cook the soaked farro in vegetable broth and water with the rosemary sprig and bay leaf over medium heat for 25 minutes until tender yet al dente, then discard herbs, season with sea salt, and press firmly into two round patties using a small ramekin or mold.
* Heat one tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil in a skillet over medium heat, add the cannellini bean tempeh cubes, and brown evenly on all sides for about 6 to 8 minutes until golden.
* Pour the aged balsamic vinegar and tamari into the skillet with the tempeh, swirling continuously until the liquid reduces into a sticky, glossy glaze that coats each cube, then set aside.
* In the same warm pan, add another tablespoon of olive oil along with the fermented garlic paste, then toss in the chopped cavolo nero and sauté for 3 to 4 minutes until wilted, rich green, and tender.
* Arrange your wooden bento box into organized sections: lay the warm pressed farro patties on one side, pile the balsamic-glazed tempeh cubes alongside, and nestle the vibrant pink and green lacto-fermented giardiniera into the center compartment.
* Fill the remaining space with the wilted cavolo nero, scatter with toasted pine nuts, and finish with a quenelle of cultured almond ricotta topped with rinsed capers.
* Drizzle a final swirl of fresh extra virgin olive oil and a pinch of flaky sea salt across the entire bento before serving.

Take this bento out into the fresh air, unhurried and present. Each compartment brings its own rhythm of acidity, crunch, richness, and depth, celebrating what happens when simple garden staples are allowed to slowly ferment and shine. Buon appetito!
