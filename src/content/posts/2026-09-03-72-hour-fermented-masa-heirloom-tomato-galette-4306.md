## Introduction

There is a quiet magic that happens when you let dough breathe and mature. Taking inspiration from the aged pastry trend hitting the artisanal bakeries of the Mission District, I have combined my love for sourdough with the deep, earthy soul of nixtamalized masa. This crust ferments for three days, developing a complex tang that perfectly anchors the sweetness of sun-drenched heirloom tomatoes and smoky peaches. It is a slice of the California coast—rugged, golden, and deeply flavored.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup active sourdough starter
- 1 1/2 cups organic all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup heirloom blue or yellow masa harina
- 3/4 cup cold-pressed solid coconut oil or vegan cultured butter
- 1 teaspoon sea salt
- 2 tablespoons ice-cold verjus or apple cider vinegar
- 3 large heirloom tomatoes, sliced thick
- 1 ripe Ojai peach, thinly sliced
- 1 Fresno chile, seeded and minced
- 2 tablespoons Sonoma olive oil
- 1 bunch fresh purple basil and cilantro
- Flaky sea salt and cracked black pepper
- 1 teaspoon smoky adobo sauce for brushing

## Instructions

- In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, masa harina, and sea salt. Cut in the cold fat until it resembles coarse crumbs.
- Fold in the sourdough starter and ice-cold verjus. Knead very gently until a shaggy dough forms.
- Wrap the dough tightly and refrigerate for 48 to 72 hours. This aging process allows the sourdough and masa to develop a deep, fermented flavor profile.
- When ready to bake, preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C). If you have a baking stone or steel, use it to mimic a wood-fired floor.
- Roll the aged dough out on parchment paper into a rough 12-inch circle. The edges should be rustic and uneven.
- Layer the tomato and peach slices in the center, leaving a 2-inch border. Sprinkle with the minced Fresno chile and a drizzle of olive oil.
- Fold the edges of the dough over the fruit, overlapping where necessary. Brush the crust with a mixture of olive oil and a drop of adobo sauce for a smoky glow.
- Bake for 30-35 minutes until the crust is deeply golden and the tomatoes have slumped and concentrated their sugars.
- Top with fresh herbs and flaky salt immediately before serving.

Take this galette outside, find a patch of shade under an oak tree, and share it with friends. The way the aged masa crust shatters against the juicy tomatoes is why I love cooking in the Bay—it is simple, honest, and purely of the earth.
