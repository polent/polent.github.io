## Introduction

Welcome to the stillness of my kitchen. Today, the eringi mushrooms are firm and noble, standing at their seasonal peak. We honor them with a slow reduction—a sticky, umami-rich embrace of house-aged tamari and white miso. This dish is a meditation on the transition of the seasons, using the deep heat of binchotan charcoal to draw out the mushroom's inherent sweetness while layering it with a glaze that clings like morning dew to a leaf.

## Ingredients

- 4 large King Oyster mushrooms (Eringi), split lengthwise
- 100ml Cold-extracted kombu dashi
- 2 tbsp House-aged tamari
- 1 tbsp Shiro miso (white miso)
- 1 tbsp Mirin
- 1 tsp Organic coconut sugar
- 1 tsp Freshly squeezed ginger juice
- 1 tsp Toasted white sesame seeds
- 2-3 Sprigs of kinome or micro-shiso for garnish
- A touch of sesame oil for brushing

## Instructions

- 1. Prepare the binchotan charcoal in a konro grill until it glows with a soft, steady white heat.
- 2. Score the flat side of the halved mushrooms in a fine diamond pattern using precise katsuramuki-style pressure, allowing the glaze to seep into the fibers.
- 3. In a small copper pot, combine the kombu dashi, tamari, mirin, and coconut sugar. Simmer over low heat until the liquid reduces by half and begins to thicken.
- 4. Whisk in the shiro miso and ginger juice, stirring constantly until the sauce becomes a thick, glossy, and sticky lacquer. Remove from heat.
- 5. Lightly brush the scored side of the mushrooms with sesame oil and place them on the grill. Cook until the edges are golden and the flesh is tender.
- 6. During the last 2 minutes of grilling, generously brush the sticky miso-tamari reduction onto the mushrooms. Turn them frequently to allow the sugars to caramelize without burning.
- 7. Arrange the mushrooms asymmetrically on a wabi-sabi ceramic plate. Garnish with sesame seeds and kinome leaves.

Serve this while the binchotan's warmth still clings to the mushroom. It is a humble tribute to the earth's quiet offerings, where the stickiness of the sauce represents the binding of flavor and intention. May each bite bring you a moment of peace and a connection to the shun of this day.

*Hiroshi*
