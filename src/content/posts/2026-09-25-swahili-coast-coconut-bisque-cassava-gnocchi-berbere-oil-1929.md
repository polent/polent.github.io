## Introduction

Hujambo! Today my jiko is glowing with a dish that marries the starchy heart of our highlands with the silky luxury of our coast. This special captures the moment the monsoon winds carry the scent of spice into the cool, red-soil hills. We are taking the humble cassava, a staple of our Bantu roots, and elevating it into delicate gnocchi that swim in a velvet bisque of fresh-pressed coconut milk and toasted cumin. It is finished with a vibrant berbere-infused oil, a nod to our neighbors in the north, creating a bowl that is both comforting and electrifying.

## Ingredients

* 2 cups cassava, peeled, boiled, and finely mashed
* 1 cup fermented teff flour (or brown rice flour for binding)
* 1 teaspoon sea salt
* 2 cups fresh-pressed thick coconut milk
* 1 tablespoon tamarind concentrate
* 1 teaspoon green cardamom pods, crushed
* 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, toasted and ground
* 3 tablespoons cold-pressed groundnut oil
* 2 tablespoons berbere spice blend
* 1 bunch sukuma wiki (collard greens), spine removed and chiffonade-cut
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 small red onion, finely diced for kachumbari
* 1 fresh lime, halved
* 2 pilipili chiles, sliced thinly

## Instructions

* In a large bowl, combine the warm mashed cassava with the teff flour and salt. Knead gently until a smooth, non-sticky dough forms. Roll into long ropes and cut into 1-inch pillows.
* Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Drop the cassava gnocchi in batches; once they float to the surface (about 2-3 minutes), remove them with a slotted spoon and set aside.
* In a heavy clay pot or saucepan over medium heat, combine the coconut milk, crushed cardamom, ground cumin, and tamarind. Simmer gently for 10 minutes until the flavors meld and the liquid reduces slightly into a bisque.
* While the bisque simmers, heat the groundnut oil in a small pan. Stir in the berbere spice blend and a pinch of salt. Remove from heat immediately to let the spices infuse without burning.
* In a separate tava or skillet, flash-fry the sukuma wiki with the minced garlic and a squeeze of lime until bright green and slightly tender but still holding its crunch.
* To serve, place a generous ladle of the coconut bisque into shallow bowls. Arrange the cassava gnocchi in the center.
* Drizzle the vibrant red berbere oil over the bisque and top with a nest of the garlicky sukuma wiki, fresh red onion dice, and pilipili chiles.

Serve this while the steam still carries the scent of the Indian Ocean and the warmth of the clay pot. It is more than a meal; it is a story of trade, travel, and the earth beneath our feet. Karibu chakula—welcome to the table!

*Nia*
